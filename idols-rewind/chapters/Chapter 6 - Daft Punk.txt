Chapter 6 - Daft Punk


*Daft Punk?*

I was scrolling through the community board on Tweetie, the streaming platform.

"Fan edits already?"

It wasn't Yoon-jung's account. Just a regular viewer who'd clipped the highlights from her first stream and posted them. The post had already racked up a solid number of views and comments.

— Huh? lmaoooo a pretty idol girl is actually Lü Bu, the most feared warrior in Romance of the Three Kingdoms?? THE CHAOS
— nobody saw that coming lmao
— lol

And Blue Sherbet's name was starting to show up too, one mention at a time. Someone had dug up Yoon-jung's fancams and screenshots from her music videos and added them to the post.

"You can spot a great tree by its first leaves, huh."

It wasn't just something people said about me.

Everyone was already seeing her for what she was.

Jung-han spoke up.

"Boss. Nothing more to look at here. Let's get a separate YouTube channel going and find her an editor."

"Do it."

He was right. Nothing more to see. Left to her own momentum, she'd be fine.

The only thing left to build was the dancing.

But that couldn't be rushed.

She was already exceptional. But she needed to reach the level where international fans would come in waves too.

Of those ten million subscribers, most of them were going to come for the cover choreography and original routines.

To pull in numbers like that, she had to dance at a level that stopped people cold.

Even people with no frame of reference, who couldn't tell a hard move from an easy one, had to feel it in their chest anyway.

And for that to happen, she had more work to do.

I was still checking the comments when a message came in from LOAN.

*[Boss, I've sent the track to your email. Please take a look and let me know if anything needs adjusting.]*

I opened the email with my heart beating a little faster than usual. The concept notes had been fleshed out from what we'd discussed, and the file was attached.

I skimmed the notes and downloaded the track. Hit play.

"He said he'd give it everything he had. Looks like he meant it."

The guide vocal was LOAN's own voice, but following the melody wasn't hard.

It had shifted slightly from what Hee-yeon had sung in the session. And the sound was a clear step up.

"Let me know what needs fixing? There's nothing to fix."

The song had been written around Hee-yeon's voice from the start. He'd taken that and wrung every last bit of quality out of it, and now I was supposed to tell him what to change?

Anything I touched would only break it.

The track was that good, and something in me, the sharp part that had never really been wrong, was saying loudly that this one would land.

I wrote back.

*[Perfect. We'll attach lyrics and start training. I'll keep you updated on progress, so please start preparing for recording.]*

*[Thank you so much, Boss. I will never forget this for the rest of my life.]*

I almost laughed.

*[I'll make sure your freelance work runs smoothly, so don't go signing anywhere else.]*

I waited a while. No reply came.

***

I booked a session with the best vocal trainer in the business and went with Hee-yeon.

The voice needed to be shaped around the track.

The trainer listened to the song, seemed to get a feel for it right away, and went straight into the session. I watched from the side.

Then he stopped, looking a little thrown.

"Her voice already fits the song. There's really nothing I'd change."

"Is that right?"

I couldn't stop the smile. It felt exactly like watching someone compliment your kid.

Hee-yeon, though, looked unsatisfied.

"Boss, no. It's not there yet. Teacher, please — a little more."

What a perfectionist.

Not long ago she'd been talking like failure was always a possibility. And now she was chasing perfect.

I decided to back her up. I had to — it was too admirable not to.

"Teacher, isn't there something we could push a bit further?"

The trainer thought about it, then slowly shook his head.

"If the lyrics were done, maybe. But without them, all I can do is keep building her technique. And that's separate from actual recording prep."

Even established artists train every day to stay sharp. It wasn't a matter of a session or two.

The point was, there was nothing specific to work on for this song yet.

"I see. Then we'll come back once the lyrics are ready."

"Yes, that would be better. I have to say — your eye for talent is something, Manager. The reputation isn't for nothing. The pairing of the voice and the song, the way it all fits. I'm excited to see this one through."

He smiled.

Something about him sat well with me.

Before the regression, I'd worked with a trainer I knew from my HS days. That one had fed every detail about our songs straight back to HS Entertainment.

Nothing came of it directly. No real damage. But I'd never trusted him again after that.

I'd looked for someone new, and I was glad I had.

Once the company's finances improved, I'd bring this one in properly.

The ambitions were growing.

I had no intention of reining them in.

For now, it was just a matter of staying focused on what the company could actually handle, and saving everything else for later.

*I need to sign more artists, too.*

All the people I'd watched rise in the future. The ones who became superstars. And the ones who had every bit of talent needed to get there, but never found the right project. Never got to bloom.

I was going to take all of them.

We were out in under thirty minutes, and I dropped Hee-yeon back at the practice room.

Back at the office, I put in the lyrics commission and had nothing pressing after that, so I reviewed the plan again and timed my visit to the dorm for when everyone would be back.

Which meant waiting for Jin-ah. She always stayed latest, per the schedule I'd set.

"Oh, Boss! You came!"

"Hey, Jin-ah. Haven't been back long, right?"

"...Boss, what are you doing? Are you sniffing me?"

She was looking at me like I was some kind of creep.

By this point it was practically a reflex. Every time I saw her, I found myself wondering whether she'd showered. You couldn't tell just by looking.

I redirected.

"Everyone home?"

"Yeah, everyone's here. But what'd you think? The smell?"

"...Huh?"

She was looking at me expectantly. A second ago she'd been calling me a creep.

What was she after?

"Hmm. You showered."

"I shower every single day!"

"Sure."

"I changed my hand cream. It smells good, right?"

She pressed her palm directly against my face.

Her hand filled my entire field of vision.

"It's sweet. Kind of like a snack."

"...Don't sniff my fingers. Try the back of my hand! The back smells good, right?!"

"Yeah, it's nice."

I answered on autopilot and went to knock on Yoon-jung and Yumi's door.

It opened right away and Yumi stepped out.

"Oh, Boss! Yoon-jung's streaming right now."

"Is she?"

I eased the door open and slipped in, staying out of the camera's frame.

She was at her desk with her headset on, locked into the screen.

Chat was going constantly.

Two days in and she didn't look nervous at all.

"Oh, the AWM! That's one of my favorites."

The chat, already busy, absolutely flooded.

Way more than yesterday.

— Huh? Full kit!
— Huh? Double barrel AND the AWM, she's fully loaded lmaooo
— Huh? Everybody sit down. Lü Bu is in the lobby.
— lol

Around seven hundred viewers.

Yesterday's regulars, plus everyone who'd come curious after seeing the community posts.

Some of this had to be temporary attention. Her looks, the fact that she was an idol, yesterday's highlights all making for a good hook. How many of those seven hundred would still be there in a month, nobody could say.

*Somehow I don't think the drop is that bad.*

I watched her a little longer, then slipped back out with a quiet smile.

In the hallway, Jin-ah was staring at that smile.

"What?"

"Nothing."

I left her to it and turned to Yumi.

"Yumi. How's the arranging going?"

"Trying not to limit myself to any one genre makes it kind of hard. But I'm working at it!"

"Can I hear something?"

"W-what? It's not good yet!"

"That's fine. I just want to listen."

She shifted on her feet, clearly uneasy, then nodded.

I already knew she couldn't be that far along yet. She'd barely started. In the future, she'd told me it took time: just doing arrangement after arrangement, no genre limits, until one day something clicked and it started flowing the way she wanted.

But it was worth checking. You never knew with this group.

She might already be further along than I expected. Maybe not with that full click yet, but the technical side might already be there.

Yoon-jung had been like that.

Yumi handed me a pair of earphones plugged into her phone and hit play when I put them in.

She wasn't looking at me. She was staring at a spot on the wall, clearly bracing herself.

Then the music started. And I went still.

"Daft Punk?!"

I told her not to limit herself, and she'd gone straight to the textbook of EDM.

Not just any track, either. "Technologic." A massive hit, one of the most covered songs in the genre.

Yumi seemed to be saying something in the background, maybe trying to explain, but with the earphones in I couldn't hear her.

I focused on the music.

Every unexpected turn in the arrangement made the smile on my face spread a little wider.

The track ended. I pulled the earphones out.

"That's good. But how did you do this? I figured you'd just be strumming guitar and recording."

"You told me to try arranging, so I ordered the equipment right away. I've got it in my room."

"...What?"

My expression went flat.

"With whose money?"

"Uh... well..."

"If you needed something like that, you should have told me!"

"...I'm sorry."

I'd been so focused on Hee-yeon that I hadn't been paying attention to Yumi at all.

When I thought about it, I was furious at myself.

Telling her to arrange things without any equipment — what exactly did I expect? Strum a guitar and call it a day? What was I thinking?

The frustration and the embarrassment landed at the same time. My face scrunched up. Yumi dropped her head.

I let out a long breath.

"Bring me the receipt and I'll transfer you the money. And if you need more equipment, just ask. Don't worry about the company's finances — that's not your problem to..."

I stopped.

Something had just landed.

"Wait."

"...Yes?"

"When did the equipment arrive?"

"Last night."

Her voice had gone very small, like she was confessing something.

"So this whole arrangement... you did it last night?"

"Yes."

"..."

My mouth fell open. There was no playing that off.

She'd learned the software and the basics of composition back at HS Entertainment, so getting up to speed on the equipment wasn't the issue. But putting together something at this level in that amount of time —

"Have you ever done an EDM arrangement before?"

"No. Yesterday was my first time..."

I had nothing to say.

What kind of group was this.

Every time I thought I'd seen it all, there was more.

"Yumi. Do you need any more equipment?"

Silence.

She still had her head bowed like she was waiting to be told off.

"Why are you so down about it? Hey — you did well. You really did well. I'm complimenting you. You don't need to be like this."

I spent a while getting her back to herself, and eventually got a list of everything she needed.

By the end, she seemed to actually believe the praise was real. A warm little smile had spread across her face.

Cute kid.

Then, from the entryway, Jin-ah's voice rang out.

"Boss! I'm heading back to practice! No need to drive me!"

Full volume, no warning.

I went out to the door.

She already had her sneakers on.

"What? Why? You were the last one out today. Weren't you just back?"

"Come on! Don't you know I have iron stamina? You really underestimate me. And honestly, practice was pretty lazy today anyway."

"Don't push it too hard."

"I'm telling you, don't worry."

Big grin. Confident voice.

The Yumi situation was still sitting with me. I wondered if I'd missed something with Jin-ah too.

"Jin-ah — is there anything you need?"

"Same practice every day, what would there be? I'm fine."

"Okay... you know you need to pace yourself, right? You've been training for years. You know how it goes."

"Yes, yes, I know."

"Actually — I'll just drive you."

"Boss! If you do that I'm going to feel too watched to actually practice. I'm fine, go take care of work. I'll go by myself! I'm not a little kid, and it's not like anyone knows who I am yet."

I smiled and ruffled her hair.

"Alright. It's good to see. But seriously — take it easy. Too much is worse than not enough. You know that."

"Sir, yes sir!"

Jin-ah pulled the door open and stepped out.

On the stairs going down, she gritted her teeth.
