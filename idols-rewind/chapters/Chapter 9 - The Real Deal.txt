Chapter 9 - The Real Deal

"This is... the real deal."

The bust shot, catching her from the shoulders up, had given all of us the same impression.

No lines. No direction. Every person in that studio catching the exact same emotion at the exact same time. That, without question, was acting talent.

The anger underneath it, slow and deep. Rooted, probably, in HS Entertainment's interference, in the failed debut, in a world that wouldn't even give them decent weather.

The grief beneath the anger: grief for the future where the members scatter and the company falls apart.

And holding all of it together: desperation. She stared at the moon with empty eyes, like she was begging the sky for forgiveness. Or for hope.

Hee-yeon had taken my pitiful attempt at direction and turned it into something extraordinary.

"One more time. Right now, one more time!"

"Get the close-up!"

Both directors scrambled, terrified they'd lose her while the emotion was still alive on her face.

Only after grinding all the way through to a full-body shot did they start giving her other directions. Hee-yeon hit every single one, perfectly, without hesitation.

Right. Of course. It was never that I had any talent for directing. It was that Hee-yeon's talent was extraordinary.

"Move the position!"

"Keep her out of the rain!"

A fire had lit in Eun-chul's eyes watching her.

I couldn't have been more pleased if I'd tried.

Her acting talent aside, it already felt like a guaranteed seat in a ten-million-ticket film.

When the day's shoot finally wrapped, I told everyone:

"Tomorrow's booking isn't until 2pm anyway, so eat and drink whatever you want tonight."

"..."

"..."

No cheer.

"We'd rather do that after tomorrow's shoot wraps too."

"We need to revise the storyboard."

Eun-chul and Jong-woo said it with identical looks of resolve.

Hee-yeon's acting talent.

It was staggering enough to make a full revision unavoidable, and more than enough to set both directors on fire.

I smiled.

"Tomorrow I'll make sure you eat until you burst."

"Yes. Understood."

The next day, Eun-chul had tossed the prepared storyboard entirely and was operating on pure instinct. The studio rang nonstop with excited direction and a steady stream of praise.

"Perfect! Can we try something more hopeful now? Like she just heard the moon can grant wishes."

"Ohhhh!"

"Pull in for the close-up!"

Hee-yeon had on the yellow dress she hadn't been able to wear the day before. The cheerful, airy thing seemed to catch her off guard. She scratched near the corner of her eye, shy.

That wasn't acting. That was real.

"...Shoot that. Actually. Just keep recording."

"Already on it, my friend."

"How are you even..."

"Basics."

Eun-chul, Jong-woo, and the junior crew were having the time of their lives. From the moment she put that dress on, the storyboard they'd stayed up all night building was worthless. What she was doing far exceeded whatever image they'd had in mind. So they called it as the ideas came, and the juniors jumped in with suggestions of their own, and the energy kept building.

"Her hair! Her hair, let's try it up!"

"Boss, that's okay?"

There was something very personally fan-motivated about that request.

I nodded. Do whatever you want.

Maybe because they were still students, they shot a lot of footage that would never make the cut. But the gems were stacking up alongside it.

When the shoot finally wrapped for real, they actually looked torn between heading to dinner and going straight to editing. Good lord. Directors this enthusiastic about the work.

I'd have welcomed them editing immediately, but these weren't people I was only going to see once.

"Let's grill some samgyeopsal. Once the money comes in I'll treat you to beef, but bear with this for tonight."

*(T/N: 삼겹살 — pork belly, typically grilled at the table. An affordable staple; beef is the treat.)*

"Yes!"

"When was the last time I even had meat?"

"Tonight we drink until we die!"

College students have so much energy.

Film students, though, perpetually exhausted every last one of them, apparently had somewhat less than the average.

How little had they drunk before they were already down?

I clicked my tongue and knocked back another glass of soju.

"Shame. They'll build up some tolerance after more time on set."

"Please... help me..."

"Urgh. Ugh!"

In the middle of total carnage, Hee-yeon and Jung-han, neither of whom had touched a drop, were staring at me like I was something not entirely human.

"What."

"...Your tolerance never stops being impressive, Boss."

"I've never seen anything like it."

I frowned.

"They're just lightweights. Film students run on empty all the time. And they shot two days straight."

"Boss, if you'd glance at the bottles beside you, just what you've had alone is already five. Five bottles."

"..."

"And you're sitting there looking like you haven't felt a thing. Is that a human expression, Boss?"

"What, punk?"

Jung-han slowly shook his head.

Not my fault if I genuinely hadn't felt a thing.

***

The next day, we shot the album jacket. The day after that, we received the final master, mixing and mastering complete.

"Clean, as expected."

It was starting to feel like something whole.

A smile found the corner of my mouth.

Now there was only one thing left.

Promotion. Possibly the most important part of all of this.

I'd already mapped every step, and with the weapons properly assembled, there was nothing to fear.

"Music shows: skip. We'll never get a slot."

Safe to call it HS Entertainment's doing.

We'd tried everything at the debut too, and couldn't get booked anywhere, not the public broadcast music shows, not even the cable ones.

Variety and radio were outside HS's reach, so they were technically options for getting Hee-yeon out there, but Hee-yeon had almost no on-screen presence to speak of. Put her on variety and it would do more harm than good. I ruled it out.

Even if she got some traction, variety should be kept to a minimum. Unless she really blew up, maybe.

But there was one exception.

A program that had the soul of a music show, but with just enough variety threaded through it.

"Yeol's Music Notebook."

A program HS Entertainment couldn't lay a finger on.

That the CP and the PD both poured themselves into it was one thing. The real reason was Hae-yeol Yoo, the host.
*(T/N: CP — Content Producer. The executive rank above the PD at a Korean broadcast station.)*
The position he held in the music industry was something else entirely. He ran his own agency, but the reputation he'd built before any of that was enormous.

"I'd love to see them try to block us. The blowback would be spectacular."

Well. Better not to count on it.

I took Hee-yeon and headed to KBC.

Time to fight head-on.

Walking into a broadcast station meant throwing down a gauntlet. It was announcing to anyone who noticed: *I'm back in the fight.*

Going around in person like this was becoming rarer. The industry had shifted toward SNS and email. But I had a name in this business. Showing up in person was worth a hundred messages.

If word spread to enough corners, at least a few would start paying attention.

"Hello, PD. It's been a while."

"Oh. Oh, Kang. Is that you."

A producer I passed in the corridor. I greeted him.

Visibly uncomfortable.

I ignored it, had Hee-yeon say hello too, and kept walking.

That was the polite version, honestly. Even if he wasn't going to use us right now, he was at least thinking about me.

"Yes. Hello."

A flat, dry greeting, and then he was gone, back to wherever he was going as if we didn't register.

People like him had written me off already. I wasn't on their radar, just one of the dozens who stopped them in the hallway every day. Nothing more, nothing less.

We made the rounds while we were there, greeting writers and producers with industry connections, anyone we could find. After the long loop, we finally reached the real destination.

"Oh? Kang!"

"Good to see you, Producer Kim."

He met me with a warm face. Producer Kim got so many requests every single day that he genuinely hated unannounced visits. I knew that. I came anyway.

Back when I was at HS Entertainment, he'd always booked my artists without complaint. He'd liked working with me. And since I'd been planning to go independent long before I actually did, I'd kept that relationship carefully warm.

"Blue Sherbet, it's been a while. First time since then, isn't it?"

"Yes! Hello! I'm Hee-yeon Lee of Blue Sherbet!"

"Just as lovely as ever."

"Thank you!"

Blue Sherbet had filmed an appearance on this program at their debut. I'd picked what I was sure was a winning song, and Producer Kim had liked the music and given us the slot. It hadn't worked out.

But this time, I hadn't brought something that was likely to succeed. I'd brought something that had to.

Producer Kim held out his palm. A smile that said he already knew.

"Hand it over. That's why you're here."

"It is."

I smirked and handed him the USB.

"Could've just burned it onto a blank CD. This is a lot."

"Ha. You can return the USB when we see each other again."

When we see each other again.

Meaning: at the recording.

"Confident, aren't you. I don't book people as favors. You know I decide the lineup with the writers and Hae-yeol."

"Don't you know me, Producer Kim? I'm not talking about something that'll keep the show's reputation intact. I'm talking about something that'll make people say, 'That's exactly what Yeol's Music Notebook is.'"

"That good? All right, I'll listen, you can count on that. Go on now. I might not call, so don't take it too hard."

"Of course."

Hyuk and Hee-yeon bowed and walked out.

Producer Kim watched their backs until they were gone, then clicked his tongue.

The assistant director beside him gave a thin smile.

"Looks like even he couldn't make it work with a group, so now it's the solo route. Probably brought some decent ballad. Get her on a bit of variety, then ease the group back in. Textbook. I thought he'd be different."

"...Yeah. Real ability on him, but the industry doesn't care much about that."

Producer Kim turned the USB over in his fingers.

"Still. Even without anyone getting in the way, it's a hard road."

"Probably had no other options. Isn't this just a last resort?"

"Hey. Last resort? You never know when to stop talking. How many times have I told you to watch your mouth."

"...Sorry."

"Tch. Let's just listen. Whatever else, he picked it, it won't be nothing."

Producer Kim plugged the USB into his computer. The assistant director settled back with the look of someone who already knew what they were about to hear.

But then.

"...Oh."

Neither of them said a word while the song played.

When the sound finally left the speakers, both of them turned and looked at each other.

"This..."

"Is the real deal."

It was.

He'd privately agreed with the assistant director: last resort. But this was far too strong for a last resort.

Then again. Would the man they'd known, the legendary Senior Manager Kang, now CEO Kang, really change that much because his title did?

The core of him was exactly the same.

"Play this for Hae-yeol."

"On it!"

Producer Kim passed the USB to the assistant director, then reached for an A4 sheet he'd folded into the corner of his desk.

A rough, scribbled list of potential guests.

At the very bottom, he wrote the name *Lee Hee-yeon* and underlined it. Hard, twice.
