# The Super Manager's Regression

"Manager Kang! Hurry up! What's taking so long?"

"Sorry, Director Park. Had to grab some umbrellas."

I brushed the raindrops off my shoulders and leaned three long umbrellas against the wall.

A glance at the table told the whole story. Two empty soju bottles between the two of them, and the flatfish was already half gone.

"It's raining out there?"

"Just a little. I was on the phone with the kids and felt a few drops starting to fall, so I picked some up from the shop out front. One for you, one for Team Lead Na."

"Long umbrellas... nice. I always preferred the long kind to those short ones. Same goes for a career, right? Longer is better."

Director Park fixed his eyes squarely on me.

Team Lead Na snorted softly and said, "Director Park. That's not going to work on him. The man turned in his resignation today."

"Is that so? No, well... you never know. Manager Kang. Ah, wait, that's not right anymore. Hyuk. You're going to start your own company out there, aren't you?"

I scratched the back of my head. Everyone asked the same question, but they were already certain of the answer before they asked it.

"Yes. I'm going to give it my best shot."

"Well, sure. With talent like yours, who wouldn't want for more? Especially that eye of yours. Absolutely killer instinct for spotting talent. And everything else you do is top-notch too. Is there another manager in this entire industry who gets called a super manager? But listen, Kang. Think it over one more time."

Director Park let the last words land softly, almost casually. Trying to hold on to me again. I'd lost count of how many times this made.

*So that's why he called a second farewell dinner. We already had one yesterday.*

Unlike the crowded affair the night before, tonight's "farewell" was just three people. He knew I was close with Team Lead Na, so he'd arranged this supposedly casual gathering to make one last play.

They didn't call him the Pitbull for nothing. Once his jaws locked on, they didn't let go.

I glanced sideways at Team Lead Na, silently asking for help. The most trustworthy person in this company, and the one I respected most. No matter how much groundwork Director Park had laid, surely the Team Lead would be on my side.

But.

*He's playing innocent...?*

Was this because I'd already left the company? Or did Team Lead Na also want me to stay?

Na read my eyes perfectly and kept his mouth firmly shut. Only Director Park kept talking.

"I told you I'd make you a Team Lead, didn't I? I'll set up Team 4 for you. Build it however you like. What's the difference between raising talent out there versus raising them here? It'll actually be easier here. You can chase whatever ambitions you want. I'll make sure nobody touches you. I can guarantee that. And I won't interfere either. I mean it."

Team Lead Kang now, was it? A moment ago it was "Hyuk."

But here was the thing. I liked the sound of "CEO" better. No matter how much ability I showed here, the highest I would reach would be director. Unless I was the one promoting Park to an executive position, I'd cap out at Team Lead.

"I'm sorry. I truly appreciate the offer, but I want to go independent."

"...That so?"

Director Park let out a dry laugh, then reached for his glass. I quickly grabbed the soju bottle to pour for him.

But a firm hand blocked him.

I looked up to find Director Park's face gone cold. He took the bottle right out of my grip as the temperature in the room plummeted.

He poured his own glass and drained it in one pull.

"Ahh. Manager Kang. You know the saying? You can tell a tree will flourish from its first leaves."

He wasn't really asking if I knew it, so I kept my mouth shut.

"How about this one? The awl in the sack. A sharp point that pokes through no matter what."

The sprout and the awl. Those were meant to be me.

"And... a protruding stone gets struck by the chisel?"

"...!"

"Director Park!"

Park glanced sideways at Team Lead Na, then turned back to me.

"You've made yourself too well known. Shown too much of what you can do. You think people won't be on guard? Think about what happens to a lion cub that leaves its mother. What do the hyenas do to it?"

Anyone listening could tell he was warning me. Persuasion hadn't worked, so now it was threats.

I forced my voice to stay level.

"Even a cub can hold its own against anything but the mother lion or its own brothers. That's why it leaves."

I meant it as a plea. Go easy on me.

"Even when its brothers told the cub they need him? Even after they begged him not to go?"

No chance, he was saying.

Director Park's words landed with real weight. His position as director at HS Entertainment, one of the biggest agencies in the industry, was enormous. Especially to someone like me, who was about to launch a company from nothing.

But if a single threat could change my mind, this dinner would never have been needed in the first place. I'd have been running Team 4 a long time ago.

I stood up from the table without a word either way. Team Lead Na covered his face with one hand and let out a long breath. It was clear now. There was no place left for me here.

"Better to leave than to stay and tear each other apart inside the same fence."

"...!"

"I hope someday we can help each other from our own fences."

Director Park's gaze was ice cold.

I looked at Director Park, then at Team Lead Na, and bowed deeply at the waist. A full ninety degrees.

I grabbed one of the umbrellas and stepped outside. Rain was pouring down in sheets.

"Lion, my ass. I was the only lion among all those hyenas."

I walked through the rain with the corners of my mouth curled upward.

The next day, instead of taking the contracted artists whose deals were about to expire, I walked out of the company with only four trainees. It was my way of asking them to look kindly on what I'd left behind. If I wanted to, I could have poached any number of the reliable cash cows.

***

"Haaa."

I sat on the wooden bench of my rooftop room, staring up at the bright full moon. Three empty soju bottles surrounded one half-finished, with a torn-open bag of snacks beside them.

"Turns out I was just another hyena. And here I thought I was a lion."

How did it come to this? My situation was nothing but pathetic.

Someone once said that between B and D, between Birth and Death, there's C. Choice.

What if I had accepted the director's offer early on? Where would I be right now?

"At least not sitting here feeling sorry for myself."

The full moon still shone bright overhead, but I had lost all my light, shrunken down to nothing.

Super manager, my ass. I went from has-been to straight-up scrap.

What good was talent when you couldn't move an inch before being crushed under the giant's heel?

"Oppa."

A voice came out of nowhere.

I turned to look behind me.

"...Hee-yeon? How... what are you doing here?"

My eyes must have been trembling.

Before me was the kind of glamorous looks and presence you would never see in a neighborhood like this. Then again, it wasn't just this neighborhood. She was one of the biggest stars in the whole country.

"Mind if I have a drink with you?"

Hee-yeon Lee slipped off her shoes and climbed onto the bench, sitting with perfect posture. The contrast was jarring. One of the nation's biggest stars, sitting in this shabby place. And somehow, by being here, she made it look like it wasn't shabby at all.

"...Why are you here?"

"Am I somewhere I shouldn't be?"

Natural as anything, like we'd seen each other yesterday. It had been years since the last time.

"Yeah, you shouldn't be here. A star like you has no business being in a place like this."

"I'm a bit late, aren't I? I came to repay my debt. I figured I'm established enough now to protect my people. You ended up like this because you were trying to protect us. And... I missed you. No matter how hard I looked, I couldn't find a single person like you."

I let out a hollow laugh. I still couldn't face any of them, and she was talking about owing me something.

"What debt? The one where I pulled you all out and made you succeed years later than you should have?"

"I've never once thought of it that way. Everything that went wrong was because of those people. You did everything you could, oppa."

"In this world, results are all that matter."

"No. Everyone else in this industry might think that way, but you're someone who values the process. I know that."

"And you don't think that's exactly why I failed?"

"No. You weren't wrong, oppa."

Stubborn as ever. All these years and she hadn't changed one bit.

I shook my head slowly.

"Fine. You're right about everything. But if you want to repay that debt, then disappear from my sight right now and never come back. That's how you repay it. Watching you keep succeeding out there... it dilutes the guilt a little."

My words didn't put so much as a crack in Hee-yeon's expression. She picked up the glass I'd been drinking from, poured herself a shot, and knocked it back. She set down the glass, then spoke.

"Let's start over. Stop letting your talent rot like this. Everyone you ever picked? Looking back now, every single one of them proved you right. Songs, projects, everything you chose ended up being recognized. It was like that from the beginning, back when we were trainees. It always felt like you could see right through everything."

"If I really could see through everything, would I have ended up like this?"

She was right, though. I did have an uncommon eye for songs, for projects, for people. Whether the public would love something or not, whether it would be a flash in the pan, whether someone had real talent, whether their character was solid. I could see it easily. That's why I thought success would come easily too. But having the eye didn't solve everything.

"Don't think so negatively. You have an extraordinary gift."

"No, I've been wrong plenty of times."

"That's because the management fell short."

"Like how I couldn't make it work even with all of you?"

A bitter laugh escaped me. Did Hee-yeon see me as someone drowning in self-pity? I almost hoped so. I wanted her to think, *He's really finished,* and walk away.

"I'm successful now, oppa. But I'm not happy about it. There's nowhere for my heart to rest. No you, no members. It feels like I'm completely alone."

"Sounds like a full-stomach problem."

"Back then, even when we were hungry, at least we were together."

"Everyone romanticizes the past. And that's just memory playing tricks. Ask anyone to actually go back and they'd never do it."

"What about you? How do you feel?"

I smirked and looked around.

"Take a look for yourself. I can't imagine there was ever a time worse than this. And for the record, this isn't nostalgia talking."

"That's exactly why I'm saying let's try again."

"Did you not hear what I just said? Disappear and never come back. That's how you can repay the debt."

"I heard you. But you said you feel guilty toward me, right? Then pay it back this way. Show me your talent. Show me an image of you standing on your own two feet. Don't just watch me on a screen in the corner of a room. What is that? Way to be irresponsible. It's just self-satisfaction."

She got a lot more eloquent since the last time I saw her. Right. Some things stay the same and some things change. Enough time had passed.

But my mind hadn't changed.

"Go home."

"No."

"I don't know about the past, but there's nothing I can do for you right now. You're already a top star, and I'm... this."

"The guilt..."

"Word games aren't going to cut it."

I locked my eyes on hers with a look that brooked no argument. Her gaze wavered, just slightly.

"Thank you. For thinking of me."

"...I didn't say I was giving up. I'll be back."

"Don't come."

"I will."

She left, stubborn to the end. And I would also be stubborn to the end. She had her life to live, and I had mine. It had been too long, and we had drifted too far apart for us to dwell on the past.

I tipped back the rest of the bottle in long, heavy gulps.

"Phew!"

Too bitter. It hadn't tasted this bad just a few minutes ago.

I flopped backward onto the bench. A soju bottle rolled off the edge when my foot caught it, clinking across the ground below, but my eyes stayed fixed on one thing only. The full moon, burning bright above me.

"Self-satisfaction?"

She was wrong. There was no satisfaction in living like this.

Years had gone by and I still ached with regret over the past. I shouldn't have been so foolish. I knew that now.

"I miss those days too, Hee-yeon. If I could go back... I really think I could get it right."

If I could go back... I feel like I wouldn't have failed like that.

As my eyelids grew heavy, my consciousness slipped away.

And in that moment, the light of the full moon shining on Hyuk Kang's body shimmered with a mysterious glow.
