Chapter 4 — A Born Composer


LOAN.

He was a genius composer with a rare gift for idol music.

Years down the line, HS Entertainment would discover him, pull him into the spotlight, and give his songs the platform they deserved. He'd repay them with total loyalty. The guy who would eventually rise to head producer before he was even thirty was probably out there right now, scraping together cash from part-time jobs and making music on the cheapest gear he could find.

'I don't know his number or where he works, but SoundCloud fixes everything.'

The town hall of amateur composers. The one place every aspiring musician eventually posted their work, without exception.

SoundCloud was the world's biggest music-sharing platform. Plenty of people had turned their whole lives around starting from right here.

So of course he'd be on it.

I typed "LOAN" into the search bar.

"There it is."

Four tracks uploaded. I listened to them one by one.

Without a single flicker of reaction.

"There's always a reason it takes a genius like this so long to get discovered."

He was the type who needed to compose alongside a vocalist. That was the only way he produced anything real. Unknown as he was right now, these four tracks were all he had to show for it. He hadn't even figured out his own style yet, so the idea of finding a vocalist to work with hadn't crossed his mind.

I sent him a message.

[This is Hyuk Kang, CEO of K Management. I'd like to commission a song and was hoping to reach out. I'll leave my contact information — please get in touch at your convenience.]

Less than a minute passed.

Bzz—

My phone vibrated.

The corner of my mouth pulled into a slow grin.

"This is Hyuk Kang."

It felt like picking up a diamond off the side of the road.

***

I talked LOAN out of coming to the company and took Hee-yeon with me to Uijeongbu instead, where he lived. There was no way to do real composition work at the office.

A small studio apartment behind the local police station. He used it as both workspace and home.

The walls and windows were completely covered in soundproofing material. The room held a computer, composition equipment, a clothing rack, and a mattress — nothing else.

"Hello. I'm Hee-yeon Lee from Blue Sherbet."

"Hyuk Kang."

No answer.

He was completely transfixed by Hee-yeon's face.

To think — the man who would one day run the idol industry was standing there in his studio looking like a deer in headlights.

"Ahem."

"Oh—! S-sorry. I'm, um— I go by LOAN. Aspiring composer!"

"Nice to meet you."

And here he was, the future heavyweight producer, in his most wide-eyed, unpolished form.

Win this person over, and I'd never have to worry about songs again.

We didn't have a studio set up at the company, and money was tight — there was no way to bring LOAN on staff the way HS might have. But if I couldn't have him, I could make sure no one else could either.

I planned to give him every piece of advice I had about building a freelance career.

If he went that route, he'd grow his reputation steadily while keeping K Management at the top of his list. HS would be the one stuck waiting in line.

'Then again, if HS decides to interfere with our work, they'll end up making an enemy of LOAN in the process.'

By the time they finally understood what they were dealing with, it would be far too late.

Every hit LOAN had churned out for HS — all those songs, that whole catalog — would be lost to them forever.

'Two birds with one stone. Three birds. The whole flock.'

I let myself imagine it: a full Blue Sherbet album, every track written by Yumi and LOAN.

Just thinking about it was enough to put me in a good mood.

"Um... you're really sure you want to commission a song from me?"

"I am. Hard to believe?"

"Well... yes. There are so many people out there more skilled than me, even just among amateurs."

No confidence at all. Classic for a creator who hasn't broken through yet.

In moments like this, you build them up.

"You might not know this, but I walked away from HS Entertainment even after they offered to build an entire team around me. My judgment is something I trust completely. Blue Sherbet's debut didn't go the way I wanted — things got complicated — but my eye for talent hasn't changed. And that eye is what led me straight to you. If there were any amateur out there better than you, do you think I'd be standing here right now?"

LOAN's nostrils flared. His body responding to praise it had been starved of.

I kept going.

"You have the potential to make it all the way to the top of this industry. That's not a guess. That's something I know for certain."

"Oh, wow... haha. That's... way too kind. No, no — I really don't think so. I'm not—"

LOAN didn't know where to look.

He'd understand his own talent soon enough.

Because I was going to make sure of it.

"Hmm. Is there somewhere to sit..."

"Sit here!"

"The computer chair is for you. You're going to be composing."

"...What?"

I dropped straight down onto the floor.

LOAN just stood there, blinking.

"Hee-yeon already warmed up her voice on the way here. She's going to sing whatever comes to her, and you compose whatever comes to you in response. Oh — we're thinking pop-influenced Korean pop, if that works."

The concept we were going for sat right between idol music and pure artistry.

Minimal choreography, but a sound every bit as polished and contemporary as any idol group — something that could reach listeners of every age.

"R-right now?! I'm not ready—!"

I knew exactly what I was asking. As a client, this was about as unreasonable as it got.

In design terms, it was the equivalent of asking someone to make something that was "fresh but trendy, clean but eye-catching."

"Actually, I think you'll find it doesn't matter. I listened to all your tracks. You're someone who needs to compose alongside a vocalist. Whether you're prepared or not isn't the issue."

I was confident he could do this.

"Me...?"

He thought it sounded absurd — but he wasn't about to let this opportunity slip away.

Blue Sherbet's debut might have flopped, but this was still a real shot at making his official debut as a composer. That meant something.

LOAN needed this.

"Hee-yeon."

"Yes."

She and I had already talked it through beforehand.

The last thing I needed was for her to look thrown off too.

"Ready to start?"

She slipped her earphones in and scrolled through her phone.

Then she took a breath, and the music came.

"Today I don't feel like doing anything."

To us, it was just her voice — no backing track. And yet it was clean, easy to listen to, entirely natural.

LOAN scrambled to open his DAW.

Then he focused with everything he had, clearly treating this as a test.

"I used to rule the world."

Bruno Mars' "The Lazy Song" gave way to Coldplay's "Viva La Vida."

The two songs had almost nothing in common. Almost — except that they were both songs Hee-yeon loved.

'Should be getting there right about now.'

Sure enough. As "Viva La Vida" drew toward its end, LOAN's eyes went wide. He started clicking.

Something had sparked. This was exactly the kind of thing that would one day earn him the title of genius — exactly what would make him HS's head producer.

He reached for the master keyboard and began to play.

Click. Click. Click. Click.

That was all I could hear.

Hee-yeon had her earphones in, and LOAN had his headset on — one ear free so he could catch her voice.

I had no idea what he was building.

But I'd seen Hee-yeon and LOAN work together before. In the original timeline — and when they did, audiences went completely out of their minds.

The scale back then and the scale right now were worlds apart. But the core was the same.

I sat there filled with something that felt a lot like anticipation.

Meanwhile, Hee-yeon kept singing without so much as a tremor. Completely unfazed by the fact that LOAN had found his spark.

'...She really does make anything sound good.'

LOAN was remarkable. But so was Hee-yeon.

I'd told her to sing whatever she liked, and she was moving through wildly different genres without breaking a sweat.

Her eyes found mine across the room.

I smiled and nodded.

Good. Keep going.

A little more confidence worked its way into the sway of her body as she kept the rhythm going.

Then came the third song.

A track from one of Korea's first great boy groups — a song that had sunk deep into the hearts of an entire generation. "Road."

An interesting choice.

Hee-yeon snapped her fingers, letting the notes fall out of her easily.

"내가 가는 이 길이 어디로 가는지, 어디로 날 데려가는지—"
[Where does this road I walk lead, where does it take me—]

And then.

"Hold on."

LOAN unplugged his headset and switched to speakers.

A rough melody line.

A synth whistle, and then layered over it — a simple, repeated violin phrase.

"...!"

"...!"

Fresh. Warm. Polished.

See? Told you.

LOAN turned to Hee-yeon. "Do you know 'Lemon Tree'? Could you try singing it?"

"Sure."

LOAN's eyes were practically spinning.

It looked like a fire had caught and was burning wide open. A talent he hadn't known was there had suddenly burst out of him, all at once — of course he was too excited to think straight.

'And he's going to be grateful to me for finding it.'

My job here was done. Now I could sit back and enjoy the show.

Time passed — how much, I wasn't sure.

The melody kept building, layer by layer, each revision pulling it into sharper focus. Hee-yeon had stopped singing other songs entirely. She was humming along to whatever LOAN asked of her now, falling into the shape of what he was making.

When it felt like enough of a foundation had been laid, I called for a break so Hee-yeon could rest her voice.

LOAN deflated into his chair like the tension had all drained out of him at once.

"Good work."

Everything I'd built my reputation on — hit songs, great works, the early signs of a star — it was all speaking clearly to me right now.

This song was going to work.

I was genuinely, deeply satisfied.

"...Boss."

"Yeah."

"How did you... know."

I'd heard this before. Too many times to count.

At HS. Before the regression. This reaction was nothing new.

I gave a small shrug and kept my mouth shut.

"I've never felt this before. It always felt like I was almost grabbing something — and then the moment I started writing, it would vanish completely. But just now... I felt like a genius."

"You are a genius. You just didn't know your own style yet."

"I'm... a genius. Talent really is... something extraordinary."

He was staring at me with so much warmth it was almost a lot.

I just smiled back.

"You're something else, Boss."

"I told you — my judgment tends to be pretty good."

"I guess you weren't exaggerating. I could tell just from hearing Hee-yeon's voice. And on top of that, you saw something in me that I couldn't even see in myself. I'll be honest — at first I wasn't sure I was ready to be debuting already. I thought my skills still had a long way to go."

LOAN stood up and bowed to me with quiet sincerity.

"Thank you, Boss. This is all because of you. I might have gone my whole life without knowing this was in me. This song... I'm going to give it everything I have."

"I believe that."

And I meant it. That kind of resolve from LOAN — how could I not trust it?

While Hee-yeon rested her voice, we drew up the contract. I transferred the composition fee on the spot.

LOAN's eyes filled with something close to joy.

In one session flat, I'd gone from most difficult client he'd ever met to most valued customer on earth.

Not that the work was finished — if anything, this was just the beginning.

Depending on the lyrics, the process might shift a little, but once LOAN delivered a first complete draft, the vocal training would start. Then we'd refine the track somewhere with better equipment. Then a proper recording studio. Mixing, mastering.

And that was just the music side. I'd still need to figure out the concept — album artwork, music video, hair, makeup, styling. And then everything that came after that.

'I'm not sleeping for a while.'

Even so, I couldn't stop smiling.

***

On the drive back to the dorm.

Hee-yeon spoke up. "Boss. Can I ask you something?"

"What?"

"Is there a reason you didn't bring LOAN on board? He seems really talented."

Honestly, even I'd been caught off guard by that.

I hadn't expected him to ask if he could come work with us directly.

He knew his own talent now — did he really feel that much gratitude toward me?

Then again — he genuinely believed he might have gone his entire life without discovering it, if not for me. That kind of debt doesn't sit lightly on a person.

"I already explained it to you. Right now our focus has to be on you. We don't have the money to set up a studio or pay LOAN a monthly salary. We're already struggling to find individual practice spaces for each of you since training's going separate ways — we can't even afford those yet. And our only artists are you four, so if LOAN's going to take on other clients, we'd need to handle the logistics of that too, and we don't have the bandwidth."

No point in pushing harder than necessary. I'd already gotten more out of this than I'd planned for.

"Still... isn't it a waste?"

I glanced at her.

She wouldn't have asked this before the regression.

She was scared.

"Why? You don't think we'll get to work with a composer like him again?"

"...You never know. Even if we work as hard as we can, what if it goes wrong again? Then it'd be almost impossible to get songs from someone like that."

"We're going to succeed. I know you've lost some faith in me after last time — that's fair. But this time. I'll make it happen. So stop worrying."

She looked at me steadily for a moment, working through whatever was in her head, and then she opened her mouth.

"I never lost faith in you. We all believe you'll get us there eventually. That's exactly why — if it does go wrong again, please don't blame yourself too much. Our failure wasn't yours alone to carry. If we'd been better, we would have made it last time too."

"...Yeah."

My hands tightened on the wheel.

She still thought the odds were against us.

Like last time.

Because she knew HS wasn't going to leave us alone.

'Go ahead and try.'

I wasn't the same person I used to be.

I had my resolve now — and nothing left to lose.

If they thought they could press down on us with that heavy hand of theirs, I was ready to grab that arm, sweep their legs out, and drive them straight into the ground.
