Chapter 7 — Are You Crying?

*Are you crying?*

Money had no way in. It had every way out.

Composition fees, living expenses, the practice room and dorm, office rent, lyricist fees — the list went on.

And that wasn't even close to all of it.

Music video, album jacket, promotions, hair, makeup, styling.

My head was spinning just thinking about it.

"God. I'm going to lose my mind."

Still, there was nothing to do but push through.

Plenty of people had thrown themselves into this industry and ended up exactly where I'd been before the regression. But get it right, and you could earn enough to put your own building in Seoul.

I knew I could get it right.

"That doesn't change the fact that I've got nothing to throw at an MV right now."

The lyrics were done. Hee-yeon was in vocal training, working to fit them.

Once that wrapped, I'd book the recording studio and move straight into the MV. The shoot didn't need to wait for mixing and mastering. With any luck, the final track would come together while we were still hammering out the concept and storyboard.

The problem was that production costs weren't something to take lightly.

"Hmm. Guess I've got no choice."

Make do with what you've got.

And what I had, instead of money, was the future.

"Right. There are directors who burn through hundreds of millions and still put out garbage. But there are directors who squeeze ten million won dry and nail the mise-en-scène."

The one I had in mind was the second kind.

There was one thing that nagged at me, though.

"He doesn't have any MV credits yet. Will he deliver what I'm counting on?"

I turned it over for a moment, then gave my head a good scratch.

No point sitting here thinking. Nothing useful was coming out of it.

Best thing to do was go and see for myself.

He'd made several short films as a student — he had the experience. A conversation would tell me what I needed to know.

The film industry hadn't taken notice of him yet. His work hadn't broken through.

But I was already on my way to Daehanyesul University.

Film Studies. That director was in the middle of his studies right now, and that's where he'd be.

***

Kim Jong-woo. Fourth year, Film Studies, Daehanyesul University.

He was slumped in the department room, dragging out one long sigh after another.

"Jjong-woo. What's with all the sighing?"

"What do you think? Huh? What do you think? Huh? What do you think, you idiot!"

"Huh?"

"What."

"...You kept saying 'huh?' so I thought you'd seen it. You haven't?"

"What are you talking about. Huh? I've got assignments burying me alive, competitions on top of that, and zero idea what I'm doing with my life!"

"You haven't seen it. Look." His classmate held out his phone. "It's a clip."

"A clip?"

Sad, scary, feel-good, questionable — if a video was worth watching, you watched it. No putting it off.

Sharing made you a good friend. Only taking made you a bad one.

Maybe that wasn't a universal rule, but it was definitely the law in Film Studies.

Jong-woo waited while his classmate pulled it up.

"Here. Watch."

"What is this — a stream? Oh... she's pretty. Really pretty."

Jong-woo gave his classmate a thumbs up. His classmate nodded enthusiastically.

"She is, but that's not even the main thing. She's actually fun to watch."

Jong-woo turned his full attention to the screen.

A few minutes passed. When the clip ended, Jong-woo's expression had gone completely soft.

"Her name's... Hwang Yoon-jung? Comments say she's in a girl group. I guess idols are gamers now too?"

"I'm done. Finished. It's a long story — look her up yourself."

"I was already planning to look her up a lot. You didn't even need to tell me."

Hwang Yoon-jung. Hwang Yoon-jung. He said it quietly to himself a few times.

Even her name was pretty, he thought.

"Hey, Jjong-woo!"

A teaching assistant had appeared in the doorway.

"What."

"The CEO of K Management is here asking for you?"

"K Management?"

Jong-woo and his classmate exchanged a blank look. Neither of them knew the name.

His classmate spoke up.

"Jjong-woo, didn't all your short films completely tank? Sounds like someone actually went looking for them."

"...Says the guy who thinks he's doing great. Some filter you've got."

"Please. I'm nowhere near great. If I were, I'd already be at Cannes. Oh, wait — you and I aren't even in the same bracket, are we?"

"Say that again."

The teaching assistant, who had been patiently enduring their usual routine, cut in.

"You two really don't know? I looked it up — the company has some nobody girl group. Blue Sherbet, something like that?"

"...!?"

Jong-woo and his classmate turned to face each other, eyes wide.

His classmate said:

"My dearest friend."

"Get lost."

***

A teaching assistant walked me to an empty classroom. I waited.

"I brought materials, but... hmm. My memory's fuzzy. When exactly did he make the shift to idol MVs?"

I sat there quietly working through it for a few minutes.

Two young men walked in.

I extended my hand to the one whose face looked familiar.

"Hello. I'm Hyuk Kang, CEO of K Management."

The two of them stared at my outstretched hand.

The one I'd reached toward shook his head.

"Aren't you here for Jong-woo?"

"...?"

The face was familiar. But apparently not Kim Jong-woo's.

"I'm Kim Jong-woo. What brings you here?"

Now I was the one staring.

"Then who is this?"

"Jong-woo's classmate. I'm a fan of Hwang Yoon-jung's — I came along to say hello."

"I'm sorry about this. I told him not to come, but he wouldn't let up." Jong-woo turned to his classmate. "Yoon-jung isn't here, so go home. I told you — there was never any chance she'd show up."

"Ha... ha... I apologize."

The classmate laughed awkwardly, gave me a quick bow, and turned to leave.

I spent those few seconds working furiously to place him, and then it came to me.

"...!"

A hundred exclamation points fired off in my head at once.

A student filmmaker who'd started turning heads with his short films. His debut feature was made on a budget so small nobody gave it a second look — and it drew two million. Then five million. And eventually, he'd go on to make not one but two films that each crossed ten million. A genuine genius.

Lee Eun-chul.

The director. The one known industry-wide for being exacting, difficult, impossibly particular — standing in front of me right now, telling me he was a fan of Yoon-jung's?

Something came out of me before I could stop it.

"Huh?"

Two voices, immediately:

"Huh?"

"Huh?"

Yoon-jung's thing. Her whole thing.

I hadn't braced for it, and my expression must have shown it — because both of their faces went scarlet on the spot. It had slipped out without either of them noticing.

I thought fast.

In the future, something like this would have been completely out of the question. But now? With him being a fan of Yoon-jung's? Maybe.

I kept Hee-yeon's name out of it and stayed deliberately vague.

"If it wouldn't be too much trouble — would the two of you be willing to direct a music video?"

"Of course!"

"Absolutely!"

Not a single question. Just yes.

What was this lineup?

Whatever doubt I'd still been carrying dissolved on the spot.

Neither Eun-chul Lee nor Jong-woo Kim was at the height of their powers yet — but two talented people working at the same time was a different story.

Something was going to come out of this. I was sure of it.

Not a masterpiece, maybe. But definitely not a disaster. And their rates would be manageable. Under these circumstances, there was no better option.

*Still — I should let them see Yoon-jung at least once.*

It was Hee-yeon's MV, but the other members swinging by the set to cheer wasn't strange at all.

"Should we sit down and go over the basics?"

As I walked them through the details, their faces dropped — and Eun-chul quietly started looking for an exit. Then I mentioned, almost in passing, that the members would be welcome on set to cheer things along. He did an immediate about-face.

And as I pulled up material on Hee-yeon, piece by piece, real fire started to come into their eyes.

*At this rate, they're going to end up fans of every single one of our girls.*

I was quietly hoping for exactly that.

Because if things went well, there might come a day when Hee-yeon was cast in one of Eun-chul Lee's films.

*Lead actress in a ten-million-viewer film. That belongs to our Hee-yeon.*

That meeting ran long. The ones that followed ran even longer.

The two directors had energy that didn't quit.

Even working with a shoestring budget, ideas came pouring out of them without stopping. Equipment and venues, storyboards, costumes, props — there was an enormous amount to lock in, and neither of them treated a single detail as throwaway.

Why would they? Student films or not, they both had real experience. They'd trained hard and studied long. And this was their first time taking on the music video format — the hunger was written all over them.

There were things I worried about. Of course there were. But talent is a force unto itself, and these two proved it — they compensated for each other's gaps, built on each other's strengths, and between them created something neither could have managed alone.

The result: I was getting completely wrung out, day by day.

"God, I'm tired."

"Boss."

"Hm? Is it over?"

Hee-yeon's vocal training had wrapped up today.

It had gone quickly — her voice and the song were such a natural match that fitting her delivery to the lyrics hadn't taken long.

"Are you tired?"

"Oh, you caught that? I'm just venting. Everybody does it — back hurts, shoulders hurt, I'm exhausted, I'm dying — you know how it is. That."

Hee-yeon's expression, as always, gave nothing away.

"No. You do seem tired. Please don't push yourself too hard. You have to take care of yourself."

But every word hit somewhere real.

Too much feeling behind it to wave off as something she didn't mean.

"I know. Thanks."

I smiled and put a hand on her head.

"Rest today. We record tomorrow."

"Okay."

Tomorrow. Recording. Finally.

***

Somewhere along the way, something in the dorm had shifted.

A feeling of momentum. Of things moving.

Jin-ah felt it clearly.

Hee-yeon unnie was neck-deep in solo prep — obviously. Yoon-jung was blowing up on her streams.

Yumi was locked into her arranging work, getting better every day, right in front of everyone's eyes.

They'd all found their thing and were running with it.

*So why hasn't he given me anything?*

Everyone had been handed something new, something shaped to fit who they were. She'd just been told to keep doing what she'd been doing.

There were days when it pulled her somewhere dark — thoughts that maybe she didn't have the talent, maybe she was holding the group back. But every time that happened she dragged herself out of it and put the energy back into practice instead.

She was someone *the* Senior Manager Kang had come for himself. She hadn't forgotten the looks on the other trainees' faces back then. The envy.

And she was being told she was doing well. Every single day.

"Okay. Just keep going. No overdoing it. Give everything."

Coming in with everyone in the morning and being the last one standing at night — alone, practicing in a quiet room — there were moments where the weight of it sat heavy. But she didn't stop.

Day after day, and then — the day of Hee-yeon's solo recording was tomorrow.

She was sprawled out on her bed when she heard the front door.

*Hee-yeon unnie?*

"Chicken's here!"

The Boss.

Jin-ah was on her feet and out her door in about a second.

"Oh! Chicken!"

"The chicken gets a bigger reaction than I do? I'm standing right here."

"Wow, honestly... how many of us are there and you brought two? I think I finally understand why you're single, Boss."

"...Truly, thank you for that."

Jin-ah giggled. Hyuk laughed too.

"Practice go well today?"

"Come on! You even have to ask? I'm known for being a practice machine."

She grinned.

"Still with the bravado."

He smiled back.

Once everyone was settled, Hyuk handed Jin-ah the drumstick.

"Good work. Keep at it exactly like that. Don't push past your limit — but don't slack off just because nobody's looking. Got it?"

"Oh! A drumstick! Boss, seriously, why are you single? You're so great?"

"Boss, I worked hard today too."

"Same."

Yoon-jung and Yumi, grumbling. Both of them with their lips out.

"Excuse me! Ever heard of respecting your elders? This is a Confucian household."

"Yumi maybe, but you and I are literally the same age."

"Yoon-jung, you made enough today to buy a hundred chickens, and you're going to make a scene over one drumstick? Go buy your own. This is my reward for my hard work, personally handed to me by the Boss himself. Not just anyone gets this. You have to be as pretty as me."

"What are you even saying. Speak a full sentence. Actually, don't speak. Ew — don't open your mouth while you're chewing!"

Jin-ah pulled apart the drumstick and let herself settle into something warm and easy.

Then Hyuk's phone buzzed.

"Hey, Jung-han."

Jung-han Yoo.

"When we head to the studio tomorrow, look after LOAN. Pick him up from his place — I'll get there with Hee-yeon by taxi. Yeah. We need to keep that relationship solid. There's a lot more we're going to need from him — Jin-ah's solo track, the group tracks, all of it. Okay."

The call kept going, but Jin-ah stopped hearing it.

She'd gone completely still. Chicken still in her hand.

*My solo track?*

She looked around at the others, wondering if she'd misheard — and found all three of them already watching her.

Hyuk hung up.

"Hm? What's with the looks? Eat."

Yumi spoke up.

"Boss — is Jin-ah unnie getting a solo?"

Hyuk nodded, face completely level, like he was confirming something that had always been obvious.

"Yeah. I told her to keep training the way she was. Once she's a bit further along, she's doing a solo. Vocal and dance, both. Timing's not fixed yet — could be the group comeback first, could be Jin-ah's solo."

"When you said to keep training the way I was... that's what that meant?"

"You didn't know?"

"How would I know that?!"

Jin-ah's voice came out louder than she meant it to.

Hyuk didn't flinch.

"You should've known! I told you that you were doing well. Why would I have you grinding away on your own if I didn't think you could carry a solo? I'd have thrown you on a radio show or a variety panel instead!"

"...Wow. Seriously."

Jin-ah didn't have words.

Something was burning in her chest that was fury and joy at the same time, with no outlet for either.

She absolutely refused to cry, so she swallowed it down hard.

"And you saying you knew I'd be good at radio and variety... you actually saw that? Somewhere?"

"Don't call me 'you' when I'm your Boss...! Wait — Jin-ah, are you crying?"

Her eyes were not cooperating.

"W-who's crying!"

Hee-yeon, Yoon-jung, and Yumi lit up at the same moment. They'd been waiting for this. Patiently. For a long time.

This was what she got for every jab, every dig, every bit of teasing she'd dealt out without mercy.

The three of them looked at each other. Slow smiles spread across their faces.

"Jin-ah. Are you crying?"

"Hey — are you crying?"

"Unnie, don't tell me you're actually crying right now?"

"I'm not! Why would I be crying, why would I!"

Jin-ah bolted to her room and locked the door. The apartment exploded behind her.

Alone in her room, tears running down her face, Jin-ah's mouth kept pulling at the corners.

*Then who do they think I am. You don't get to be Choi Jin-ah without at least that much.*
