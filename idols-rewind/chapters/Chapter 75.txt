< Hee-yeon's Obsession >

The lobby of the public hall.

Extras dressed as music show staff hurried back and forth, busy with their assigned jobs. Others hauled in equipment and gear, everything but the cameras, miming the work of sorting it into place.

In front of them, Manager Gong Sung-jin, played by Sung-hyun Park, glared at Hyun-ah and let his voice go up like a thrown chair.

The first scene Sung-hyun Park and Hee-yeon played opposite each other.

"Who do you think you are! You took Mina, didn't you. Do you have any idea how important today is for her?"

Beside Manager Gong stood Mina Lee, head down, tears dropping off her chin. Around her the other members watched Mina and cried, and there was blame in the way they looked at her.

Hyun-ah shrugged and smiled, easy as anything.

"Manager, was it? I'm Mina's friend. I know exactly how important today is. That's why I came all this way. And look at me, showing up to help the girl who once took an important day away from me. I really am a soft touch. Just wait a little. Something good is about to happen."

Manager Gong's eyes flared and he opened his mouth to fire back.

Then a crowd came through the front doors.

In the world of the film, they were the cast and crew of the nation's best-loved variety show.

"Huh? There are still idols here?"
"What did I tell you! I said they'd still be here!"
"Man, your luck is unreal. I was dead sure I had that one."
"Come on! Bwahahaha!"

The film was a comedy drama.

The comedy half showed up everywhere. A chain of events set off by luck. When bad luck bloomed, spectacularly rotten things happened and played for laughs. When someone held good luck, ridiculous opportunities came popping up out of nowhere.

*Never happens in real life.*

Which is exactly why it's a movie.

I snorted quietly and kept watching.

A rookie group landing, out of nowhere, on the show the whole country loved most. They'd scrub the tear tracks off with makeup, and that one short appearance would send them straight up. Under normal circumstances they'd have been banned from music shows too, but the luck holds, the show's chief producer and the agency's team leader end up dating, and the whole knot comes undone on its own.

"Cut! Okay!"

Early on there had been the hurdle of Mi-yeon Chu blowing take after take, but once Sung-hyun Park, Hee-yeon, and Sung-ah moved to the center of the scene, the shoot wrapped without trouble.

"Thank you for the hard work, everyone!"

Hee-yeon bowed to the staff, over and over.

I waved to the fans, who were staring at my face and then hers with something close to prayer in their eyes.

"Hee-yeon's fans, stay behind a minute."

Smiles broke out across the whole group.

They'd sprung for a coffee truck and stood outside all day. I couldn't just send them home like that.

The least we could do was a mini fan meet, right?

She'd already done something similar a few times between takes. But the ending is what people remember.

Hee-yeon and I left the crew to break down the set and headed for the front doors, back to the spot where the fans had waited earlier, sweating through their shirts.

They looked exhausted, and they were all smiling.

"Oh, it's actually cool out now."
"I had no idea a shoot ran this long."
"This was fast, honestly. I would've been happy if it went longer."
"Same. The waiting was the hard part. Watching Hee-yeon act from that close wasn't hard at all. She's really good!"
"Right? Right?"

I gathered them together and handed the baton to Hee-yeon. They sat down on the ground and looked up at her, and what came off them now ran hotter than when they'd first arrived.

*They've made up their minds about her character.*

Waiting. Catching scraps of what the crew said about her. Watching the shoot with their own eyes. All of it had settled in and gone deeper.

Hee-yeon bent at the waist, a full ninety degrees.

"Thank you for supporting me. I know it was a long day."

Every trace of tiredness left their faces as they listened.

"I could work harder because you were out here. You gave me a lot of strength."

Her expression, her eyes, the way she said it. All of it plainly, completely sincere.

The fans looked like they'd been shot clean through the heart.

Yeah. That's her.

Gets shy over nothing, then turns around and says something that embarrassing without so much as blinking.

They drifted off chewing over the moment, the way people do coming out of a concert.

Hee-yeon and I cut back through the lobby toward the car parked at the rear entrance.

I had my hand on the door when I heard it.

"Oppa."

Sung-ah's soft voice. Hee-yeon and I both turned.

Sung-ah's eyes rested on Hee-yeon for a moment, then came to me.

"Can I borrow you for a second?"
"For what?"
"Are you going to keep hurting my feelings? I just want to talk. The two of us."

Nothing about her face looked hurt. If anything, there was a faint smile on it.

I turned to Hee-yeon.

"Wait in the car."
"...Okay."

Once she was inside, Sung-ah turned and started walking.

*What's this about.*

I followed her, and she opened the rear door of her own van and got in. One glance was enough to see the inside was empty.

My eyebrows pulled together hard.

A woman who has never once worried about how anything looks, and she'd cleared out her own van to say something. What could it possibly be?

I hoped it wasn't serious.

"Are you getting in, or are you going to stand there all night?"
"Let me brace myself first. You're the one making this scary."

I let out a small sigh, climbed in, and shut the door.

"So what is it?"
"I came to warn you."
"Warn me?"

Sung-ah's eyes settled on mine. Her eyes and the corners of her mouth were both curved, which didn't match a word like warning at all.

"I'm actually starting to get nervous. Out with it."
"It's nothing huge. It's Hee-yeon. I think she's showing signs of getting fixated on you. And I don't think you've noticed."

My eyes went round.

Her smile deepened.

"So you really didn't know."

Hee-yeon was fixated on me?

I went back through my memory, piece by piece.

*Fixated, huh.*

But no matter how long I turned it over, I still couldn't see it. Which part of her was supposed to be that?

"Um...? Oppa."
"Hm?"
"Why are you... smiling?"

Was I?

I put my attention on my own face and found that I was.

Probably from replaying the things Hee-yeon did. When I realized it, a short laugh slipped out of me.

Sung-ah gave me a look like something was wrong with me. Fair enough. Someone tells you a girl is fixated on you and you sit there grinning about it.

I know how it looks.

"If you came this far to say it, you're fairly sure."
"Of course I'm sure. So why were you smiling?"

I hadn't been laughing at it. It was just a smile.

"It's cute. Blunt and cold on the outside, and not remotely that underneath. You saw her with the fans just now, right? That's her. Sometimes she'll say something that goes straight to the bone and leave you standing there."

Sung-ah huffed out a laugh at me going on like some doting father bragging about his daughter.

"What are you even talking about? What kind of brain does a person need to take obsession that lightly?"

I kept the smile where it was and looked her in the eye.

"Is there a reason it has to be serious? It's not exactly unusual."

I tipped my chin toward her and went on.

"You did it too. Kid did it. So did the others. Why get sensitive about it now? That's just what happens. You start out with nothing, you grow side by side, step by step, and somewhere in there a bond forms without either of you noticing, and then the fixation comes with it. Unless you clash as people, that's how it goes."

Something in Sung-ah's eyes tightened and released in the same instant. As if it had never moved at all.

"Mine was business..."
"The shape isn't the same for everyone. People spend their time differently. People are built differently."

Even if she was fixated, it didn't worry me.

What I had with the girls wasn't quite the standard manager and artist arrangement. They'd been there since the company started, and the way we built that bond was never confined to business.

"The girls and I spend most of the day in the same room. I'm at the dorm all the time. I'm their manager and their boss, sure, but I'm also just someone they're close to."

She looked at me like I was hopeless, so I added one more thing.

"Besides, is Hee-yeon the type to cause a scene the way you did? Worst case, she crumples a script."
"...I don't know. Are you sure?"

Tensed up for nothing. Over nothing.

"Is this what you were waiting around for?"

Her mouth curled up slightly at the question.

"Not really. The timing just worked out. My manager asked me to wait in the car while she had something to discuss with the crew. She's probably hitting on the field PD."
"...And you're putting up with that?"

Back when she was with me, it would have been unthinkable. She'd have badgered me about what there was left to talk about and told me to hurry up and drive.

"I wanted to talk to you anyway. And I told you, this kind of relationship is easier for me. There's nobody at that company I want to build a bond with."
"You've changed a little."
"I've always been like this. The environment changed."

Sung-ah's head turned toward the front. I followed her eyes. Her manager was coming toward the van with a face like she'd bitten into something rotten.

The field PD hadn't gone her way, apparently.

Or maybe she really had been talking business.

The odds of that looked very low.

"I'm off."

I got out of Sung-ah's van and into ours. Buckled the belt, started the engine.

I could feel someone staring a hole in the side of my head. I swallowed the laugh and turned to meet that straight, unwavering look.

"What?"
"What did you and sunbae talk about?"

I couldn't keep the corner of my mouth down.

"Nothing much."
"...Okay."

Her eyes came off me and went to the window.

See? Not the type to cause a scene.

**

I repeated it back into the phone.

"Sorry, K-Concert?"

K-Concert.

An annual event funded by a long list of corporate sponsors and broadcast on cable. There was merchandise, cosmetics, a dozen side operations, but the heart of it was always the concert. It was held in a different country each year, and this year it had landed in Tokyo.

Japan, of all places, and right now. Luck was doing me a favor.

Jin-ah's name had been creeping up over there lately, and I'd been watching for the right moment to make a move.

-We were hoping Blue Sherbet could take the stage.

They were fishing.

I knew they'd go for the whole group.

K-Concert had the parent company of a broadcaster with real industry weight tangled up in it, and it was a chance to promote overseas. Which meant that for a rookie group, unless there was something genuinely major in the way, you took it.

That's for ordinary groups.

Blue Sherbet isn't ordinary.

Nice try, getting them for free.

"I'm sorry. Blue Sherbet won't be possible."
-President Kang, please, just reconsider one more ti...

The writer pleaded down the line.

But now was not the time for Blue Sherbet to come back together. What would they even do out there, 'ICE' and a cover stage?

The time for my girls to stand on a stage together is a proper comeback with a new song.

Show people the reunion early and the comeback loses its punch.

I cut the writer off. Not worth a second's thought.

"I have no intention of sending them as a group. None."
-Sir, there's really no way?

Persistent.

Instead of dragging out a pointless back and forth, I laid the final answer down clean.

"Hee-yeon has to focus on the film, and Yumi doesn't have the time because of her album. Jin-ah is the only one I can send."
-What? Just Jin-ah? All right, Yoon-jung doesn't have a song, but Hee-yeon and Yumi both do!

The voice jumped in alarm.

They'd never actually believed they'd get Blue Sherbet. But from that reaction, they'd been counting on walking away with at least two.

Not a chance.

Jin-ah only.

Yoon-jung was out of the conversation with no song of her own, and Hee-yeon was at the front end of a shoot and needed to be on acting. Yumi's music was too quiet to sit right at K-Concert, so all we'd be doing is handing them the buzz and the eyeballs while we ate the loss.

I said it flatly.

"Jin-ah is all we can do. Her name is coming up in Japan right now, so she'll get a good response on her own. You already know that, don't you?"
-Haaa.... Yes.

The voice went pitiful, but that writer would swap masks depending on who was on the other end of the line. With one caller, a little quiet pressure. With another, a firm no, sorry, there are no slots left. With a third, maybe the full weight-throwing routine: we can give you exactly two minutes, take it or leave it.

None of which works on someone who's been rolling around this business as long as I have.

-Two songs, though, right? One would feel like a waste for Jin-ah too. Wouldn't it?
"Would a cover be all right?"

Jin-ah only has one released track.

-We'd love the one she performed with Hyun-hee Lee at the audition. Would that be possible?

A grin spread across my face.

"Of course."

Considering who I have to thank for Jin-ah's name coming up in Japan at all.

It had better be possible.

**

The moment Jin-ah's stage was confirmed, we called a meeting. The item on the table was Jin-ah's move into Japan.

"Let's see if we can open up some Japanese schedules. Timed around K-Concert."

Since we were doing it anyway, I'd love to sweep the whole place clean the way the Star of Asia once did.

*(T/N: "Star of Asia" is the nickname of BoA, the Korean singer who broke into the Japanese market.)*

With one song out, that was probably a stretch.

"Boss, does Jin-ah speak any Japanese at all?"

Ah. Important question.

How was Jin-ah's Japanese again?

I picked up my phone and called her.

-Yes, this is the pretty Jin-ah speaking.

Ignoring that.

"How much Japanese can you speak?"
-Sushi. Sashimi. Tempura.

I hung up.

"Let's focus on performance slots that don't require talking."
