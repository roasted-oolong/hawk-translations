# Idols Rewind — Chapter 2: The Bluff Queen

"Boss."

A voice right at his ear, soft and teasing.

He shifted onto his side, still half-asleep.

"Boss!"

"Augh!"

The next sound hit like it was trying to rip his eardrum clean off. His eyes flew open and he threw himself upright.

"Ugh—!"

"Eek!"

A face, barely an inch from his own.

He recoiled. The face's owner screamed.

"What — why would you do that! Who on earth wakes up like that?!"

With some distance between them, he finally got a proper look at her.

His mind went white. He couldn't read the situation at all.

"...Jin-ah Choi?"

She was supposed to be booked solid all week — variety, music, everything. When had she shown up here? Hee-yeon had come by last night — had she already said something to Jin-ah?

But... her face. It was so young. Like before, back when she was still under his care.

"Huh?"

He tilted his head and stared at her.

She darted her eyes around, uncomfortable under his gaze.

Lightning was going off somewhere in his head. His eyes went as wide as they could get.

His brain finally clicked back on. He looked around.

"This is... my old office."

"Old? Did you not finish waking up? You're acting really off today. Did you sleep here? That's why you weren't picking up."

He just looked at her.

A casual tracksuit. Hair in a messy bun. No makeup.

"Jung-han Oppa has the day off, so can you take me to the practice room, Boss?"

"Jung-han Yoo?"

"Yeah, Oppa. Come on. You said we're starting back today now that the promotion period's over."

Office. Jung-han Yoo. A young Jin-ah Choi. Promotion period. Practice.

The pieces snapped together.

He had gone back to the past. Not to the very beginning — to the point right after going independent, after Blue Sherbet's first debut had gone down in flames.

***

Why here, of all places?

If he was going to come back, it should have been the very beginning — right when he first went independent. Why did it land here? After the failure. After every connection had frayed and fallen apart.

"Haah."

"Why are you sighing like that."

Jin-ah was eating the ice cream he'd passed her, watching him like he was some kind of strange exhibit.

One day she'd leave him, build a name for herself as a variety star, and go on to make it as a solo artist too. Right now she was under his management.

Should he call that lucky?

Actually — now that he thought about it, the company had been called a miracle company later on. Because it somehow managed to fall apart with a lineup like this: Hee-yeon Lee, Jin-ah Choi, Yoon-jung Hwang, Yumi Cho. That was the miracle. Dozens of Blue Sherbet's fancams and videos crossed a hundred million views each. Their songs turned up on the charts every time things slowed down.

"What are the others doing? Why are you here alone."

"What do you think. They're sleeping. I came to get you, Oppa."

"...Okay. They're all asleep — so why are you here alone? It's five in the morning."

"I'm the diligent one. Everyone else is slacking. You need to work as hard as me just to stay afloat, and they're in bed?"

"Ha."

He was genuinely speechless. Who else but the Bluff Queen.

"You sleep the most out of all of them and you're calling yourself diligent? Your schedule flipped during the break, didn't it. You were bored. You knew I was here."

"I did not! I'm going to work so hard this time. For real. I swear."

Work hard. Work hard.

Why did those words hit him somewhere tender?

They had worked as hard as anyone could. Every single one of them. So had he.

And it wasn't just effort. They had the talent to match — more than anyone around them.

He'd proven that at HS Entertainment. They'd proven it later on their own.

The reason they failed was one thing: those people. HS Entertainment.

'Though I have to own some of it.'

He'd tried to take the hits he had to and avoid the ones he could. Tried not to clash. Tried not to draw battle lines.

That was the mistake. He should have gone in like he meant it — no reading the room, no holding back. Taken everything he could take and not thought twice about the fallout.

'I trusted my own ability too much. I got comfortable.'

He'd assumed success was basically inevitable. Had been walking around fully confident when he wasn't even at a major agency anymore. Had told himself he could always bounce back from a setback.

"B-Boss? What's going on with your face? I said I'm going to work hard — why do you look upset?"

"Huh? No. You should work hard. I'm not upset. Just... got a lot going on in my head."

"Should we skip the practice room?"

He paused. What shape was Jin-ah in right now? He needed to see for himself.

"Let's go."

They left the cramped Ilsan office and climbed into the old Starex, heading out to the practice room on the edge of town.

It was a sorry sight compared to HS Entertainment. Another small agency had fitted out a basement unit in a residential building and put it up for rent — that was where they'd ended up. No real ventilation. Suffocating in the summer, brutal in the winter.

This was where they had built their dreams.

The Blue Sherbet members had been stranded at HS with no word on when anything would happen — pushed aside when the company decided to put its energy into a boy group launch. When someone with an actual reputation in the industry reached out, they came. Of course they came.

And then all he'd given them was a place like this. He couldn't even make them successful. The guilt had never really gone away.

"Okay, hold on. Let me stretch first."

"Warm your voice up first. Stretch after."

"Singing's fine. I can do anything, you know that. Morning, night — singing, dancing, I'm good."

She wasn't entirely wrong, actually. This wasn't the kind of empty boasting she sometimes fell back on. Vocally, in the choreo, in front of a camera — there wasn't a real weak spot. And it wasn't like she was falling short in the looks department either. Against Hee-yeon and Yoon-jung she came up a little short — but she and Yumi had that kind of cute, openly charming presence. The type fans completely lost their minds over. Totally its own thing.

"Alright, I'm ready. What do you want to do? Oh, this kind of feels like a surprise monthly eval. I'm a little nervous, ha!"

"If it were a monthly eval you'd at least have washed your hair first."

"I washed it!"

She shouted. He knew exactly what was going on. She had the kind of hair and skin where you couldn't really tell the difference — nothing showed — and she found the whole process deeply inconvenient. Any day that wasn't a full sweat session, she'd skip it without much thought.

"Sure."

"I really did!"

"Okay. Try something for me. Beyoncé — 'If I Were A Boy.'"

"...Right now?"

Her expression locked up.

"Hmm..."

"Just try it."

"Alright. It's a hard one, but... I'll do my best."

Beyoncé's hits were standard training material for any female artist. Pulling it out without warning wasn't unfair — even Jin-ah, who wasn't strong in English, would have drilled this song enough times to know it cold. Doing it justice was the whole challenge.

She didn't look confident, but she got focused fast — like she'd decided to give it everything regardless.

He'd picked this song for a reason.

In another lifetime, a cover of it became the moment people stopped seeing Jin-ah as just a variety personality and started hearing her as a real vocalist. After that performance landed, she began building her music career alongside variety — and it held up. That cover wasn't luck.

He remembered it the same way everyone else did — burned in. Using it as a reference now, he could figure out exactly where she was in the journey and how far she still had to go.

Jin-ah stared at the lyrics on her phone. The music started.

Her voice came in with it.

"If I were a boy, even just for a day."

Clean opening. Grabbing people in the low register right off the top wasn't something he'd expect from her yet — that was fine.

Her husky voice carried it forward.

'Here's where it starts to matter.'

The first peak. More restrained emotionally than the later one, but it still needed to carry weight. In that other time — the cover that no longer existed — she had caught people right here, at this exact moment.

"If I were a boy!"

"Again."

"...What?"

"That part. Again."

"Oh. Okay!"

She worked her phone, visibly caught off guard.

"If I were a boy!"

"Again."

"Got it."

More force this time, but not quite there. Force wasn't the answer. She needed to pull — to hook you and hold on. Her potential was still locked, not ready to come out yet.

"Again."

"Again."

"Again."

Each time she got a little more serious. She listened back, tried to feel her way into it, looking for something.

And then — between one run and the next.

"...!"

A piece of what she actually had broke through the surface.

Only a piece. It disappeared as soon as she moved to the next section. But it was all there — the potential, sitting right underneath, waiting for the moment it could finally come out.

"Do I have to go again?"

"That last one was good. Hold onto that feeling and try it again."

Her whole face opened up.

...He hadn't been ready for that. It had been a while since he'd seen it. She really was cute — that wide-open, outgoing kind of charming that fans fell completely apart over. No wonder it was hard to keep your guard up.

They ran another ten minutes and broke. Even during rest she kept the music going, trying to let the rhythm settle into her body.

He sat watching her and let his mind go quiet.

"...H-hey, why are you looking at me like that?"

She went self-conscious, arms crossing over herself.

He almost laughed.

"There's nothing to cover..."

"Excuse me?!"

Full volume. Eyes blazing.

"Then why were you staring! You weren't thinking anything weird, were you?!"

"Easy. ...Hey, Jin-ah. Hypothetically."

"...What."

"Say the company goes under. The group splits up. Everyone goes their separate ways. You land at another agency, and you make it — first as a variety star, then as a solo artist. How does that feel?"

"Huh? I'd hate it but I'd be happy."

"Make that make sense."

"I wanted to be a singer, so making it would feel good. But the company failing and losing the members — I'd hate that part."

...He'd asked something obvious.

For a moment he'd caught himself worrying — coming back like this, was he somehow ruining something for them? Taking away a future they were supposed to have? He let it go. He wasn't here to let things play out the same way. He was here to get them there faster and higher. Keep the group together. Build something that actually lasted.

"Why are you saying things like that anyway. Don't jinx it. I'm going to work harder and we're going to make it this time. I mean it."

She wasn't holding him responsible. She was turning it back on herself. That kind of thinking was probably why she'd ended up succeeding in every direction she went.

"Do any of you resent me? I pulled you away from where you were and couldn't deliver."

"Resent you? No."

"Not even a little angry about how the debut went?"

"...You want me to be honest?"

He swallowed. Why was he so nervous about this? He relaxed his expression and nodded.

"Yeah. Honestly."

"It's just... kind of strange."

"What is?"

"I thought if we failed it'd feel really dark. Heavy. Sad. But it's more like — I'm disappointed, yeah, and sad — but there's still this feeling like something's possible? Like we didn't stop, we're still going somewhere? I don't really know how to say it. Is that weird? You're supposed to feel that fire after you fall and push back even harder. Maybe I'm just not made like that."

"...The others feel the same?"

"Yeah. It doesn't seem like it hit them that hard. Maybe because you haven't changed how you treat us?"

Nothing's changed?

Hee-yeon's voice surfaced from somewhere in his memory.

— I'm not happy. I don't have anywhere to put what I feel. You're not there, and the others aren't there. I feel like I'm just alone.

— That's what it looks like from the outside when someone has everything.

— Back then we were hungry. But at least we were all together.

This industry treated artists like products. And how you handled a product was supposed to change based on whether it was selling — that was just how things worked. Even the people doing the managing were no different.

Hee-yeon, surrounded by success, must have been surrounded entirely by people who saw her as a successful product. People whose whole attitude toward her came down to results. Nothing else.

He smiled, quiet and small.

— Results are what matter in this world.

— Maybe everyone else in this industry thinks that. But you're someone who cares about the how. I know that about you.

— And you don't think that's exactly why we failed?

— No. You weren't wrong.

She was right. He couldn't bring himself to throw someone away when things went badly — to treat them as if they'd gotten smaller somehow. Especially when it was on him. Changing how you treated an artist after a failure wasn't accountability. It was just passing the blame.

Everyone else in this industry did it. But not him.

So now there was only one thing left.

Prove Hee-yeon right.

Prove he wasn't wrong.
