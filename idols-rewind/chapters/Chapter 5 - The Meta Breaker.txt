Chapter 5 - The Meta Breaker

The next morning, with LOAN needing a day to himself, I was back at the office. Sitting across from me was a very large man I was staring at with considerable intensity. Jung-han Yoo — the only manager at the company. The one person I'd pulled out of HS.

"Jung-han."
"Yes, Boss. Did I... do something wrong?"

Do something wrong? No. If anything, the debt went the other way. This guy was everything — earnest, honest, loyal to a fault. I'd sold him on a future full of promise and delivered nothing but hard times. I was miserable through all of it, but so was he. The members at least landed on their feet eventually. Jung-han just lost years.

I put a hand on his shoulder.

"Jung-han. This time, I'm going to make you someone this industry actually respects."
"Boss...? Are you... thinking about gambling? Absolutely — absolutely not!"
"...What goes on in your head. I'm talking about succeeding in management."
"Oh. Right. Ha."

He hadn't betrayed me even when HS Entertainment came calling with something sweet. That kind of loyalty — I owe him for it. He could've taken the money and walked. The smart move would've been to betray me and cash out. But no. Not him.

This time, I was going to let Jung-han do everything he wanted.

"Jung-han. The plan changed."

I walked him through everything I'd worked out while he was gone. The members' potential. The contract with LOAN yesterday. The shape of what HS Entertainment was likely to throw at us, and exactly how we'd need to respond. Every piece I'd thought through, I laid out in front of him.

The longer I talked, the more something crept into Jung-han's eyes. Respect, slow and steady, spreading like it had nowhere to be.

"As expected of you, Boss! You're the one I respect most!"
"Right. Just follow my lead."
"There is one thing, though, Boss."
"Hm?"
"About Yoon-jung's plan."
"What? Does the streaming thing bother you? Don't worry — she'll put up dance videos too, show off the artist side of her—"
"No, it's not that."

Jung-han looked genuinely puzzled.

"Does it have to start later?"

"There are plenty of streamers who blow up even when they're bad at the game. Skill and popularity don't have much to do with each other."
"...Really."

Honestly, streaming wasn't my world. All I knew was that Yoon-jung was seriously good at games, and that she was going to rack up views on streaming and YouTube. That's why I'd planned to wait until her skill was at a certain level before starting. The idea that skill and popularity weren't really connected — that hadn't occurred to me.

"And honestly, Boss. With Yoon-jung's looks, she's already halfway there."
"Even for a gaming stream?"
"If she's good at the game too, that's a cherry on top. But what matters most is whether she's entertaining. Looks and skill mean nothing if she's not fun to watch. And you can't know that in advance — not now, not later."

I already knew she was going to be popular. Which meant her streams were going to be entertaining. And if that had nothing to do with her skill level — then what exactly was I waiting for?

"I think she could start now. If she's got it, she's got it. If she doesn't, better to find out early and keep the focus on dance videos. Streaming presence can improve over time, but the raw potential shows up pretty fast."

I really did have an eye for things.

Streaming, YouTube — I was handing all of it to this man from here on out. What was there even to think about?

"Then let's go get her started right now."
"Boss."
"What."
"We're also going to need streaming equipment."
"..."
"I'll handle everything. Just drop the members off at the practice room and come back."
"...Fine."

The respect that had filled his eyes moments ago was completely gone.

Yoon-jung came back from practice to find her computer desk completely transformed. Ring light, a second PC tower and monitor, a microphone, a camera.

"Boss? I'm not ready yet."
"Not ready how?"
"My skills still need a lot of work."

That morning, I'd thought the same thing. Not anymore.

"Just try it as a test run. That's all."
"...Oh. A test run."

Yoon-jung nodded. With Jung-han's help, she set up an account, learned the basics of running two monitors, and got a quick rundown on how to actually operate a stream — just the essentials.

He must have been studying up while I took the members to practice. Or — no. Actually, let's not go there. If he'd been any good at this himself, he wouldn't still be working here.

I buried the suspicion and moved on.

She launched the game and went live. Since she'd been told it was just a test, she seemed completely unbothered.

"Just play like you normally would. Since this is a stream, try saying out loud what you'd usually just think. And if you have a moment, glance at the chat. Got it?"
"Got it."

Her appearance didn't need touching up. I'd left her in a plain t-shirt — too much styling and she might tense up. Under the ring light, bare-faced and natural, she was effortlessly radiant.

*This is probably better, actually.*

The stream started slowly.

At first, Yoon-jung felt the weight of two pairs of eyes watching her. But the moment the game loaded, we ceased to exist.

Normally, Yoon-jung's daily gaming time was limited — there were plenty of days she didn't play at all. But that leash had been cut loose. She'd been playing steadily since her break period, and right now her form was at its peak.

*Say it out loud, right.*

She glanced at the viewer count. Twelve. Not many people, and it was only a test run — no pressure.

*Right. Just practice.*

The game was a battle royale — a hundred players dropped into a shrinking zone with nothing, scavenging for weapons, fighting until one person was left standing.

"The flight path goes right over Mil-Base. I'll drop here."

Military Base. The hottest drop zone on the map — most loot, most players, and the hardest place to survive.

I was caught off guard — she'd just started talking to me out of nowhere. Before I could respond, the donation sound pinged through the speakers and the AI voice read it out.

*— Huh? Boss? Test run? What does that mean? Fill us in. Huh?*

"Oh — my boss just told me to try streaming as a test run, so I turned it on."

Chat immediately filled with question marks.

Two donations in on a first stream. Rookie with a veteran's stats.

"Oh! I forgot to introduce myself. Hi, everyone. I'm Yoon-jung Hwang, main dancer of Blue Sherbet."

Chat erupted — all 52 of them, going like the feed was on fire.

"...Pretty and good at the game — cherry on top. Fun and skilled — the whole package. Active idol who can actually dance? Boss, that's a combination you only see once in a dynasty. Yoon-jung... is going to be something else, Boss."

That day, Yoon-jung streamed for five hours. She peaked at 400 viewers and earned ₩300,000 in donations.

And that was before word spread.

By the time the stream ended, clips were already making the rounds in streaming communities — post after post, all with her footage attached.

The meta breaker had arrived.
