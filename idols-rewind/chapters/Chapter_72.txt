# Chapter 72

## First Place

Jeju Island.

The faces of eight girls glowed red in the light of a campfire.

The crackling of burning logs and the hum of insects stirred something tender in the air.

"I still can't believe we're actually debuting."

Someone said it quietly, and someone else picked up the thread.

"Do you think people will like us?"

"We'll get fans and… stuff."

"Aaah! Fans!"

"What? What? We'll definitely get fans! If we don't… that's a problem."

Cameras surrounded them, capturing every moment.

Japanese members, Chinese members, and Korean members.

The staff couldn't help but smile at the fresh-faced nervousness of girls on the verge of debut.

"The leader should say something."

Everyone turned mischievous eyes on Hyun-hee.

"Why do you keep calling me the leader? She's the leader, not me."

Hyun-hee's flustered protest sent the whole group into laughter.

"You're the one who pushes us so hard in practice."

"Well… I learned a lot, so…"

It was a conversation they'd had countless times before.

Hyun-hee had taken everything she'd learned and realized alongside Jin-ah and applied it directly to group rehearsals.

The members continued talking as they stared into the campfire.

"Honestly, without Hyun-hee, there's no way we'd be this detailed."

"Our seniors are really incredible. When do you think we'll get to their level?"

"From what Hyun-hee says, they're never satisfied. Jin-ah sunbae too."

More than half of these members had trained alongside the Blue Sherbet members at some point.

Someone gazed into the campfire and murmured.

"Let's… set our goal high, too. Never be satisfied. Like those seniors."

Eight in You.

HS Entertainment's newly launched eight-member girl group.

Under Director Park's aggressive push, they'd been given everything a debuting idol group could ask for.

The best song. The best concept. The best choreography. The best promotion.

In an industry that births countless idol groups every year, a freshly paved highway stretched out before them.

***

"The firepower is insane."

Manager Jang let out a disbelieving laugh, eyes on his computer screen.

Nobody else was in the office but me.

So that remark was directed at me.

"What is?"

"M&K. Min-seok and Kid. These guys. One collab and the concept trailer is gorgeous. Photo teasers, video teasers, MV teasers, all of them dropping in multiples. Jacket making-ofs, MV making-ofs, commentary, special clips. The bait is just overflowing and they haven't even started promotions."

Right. That news. Of course I knew.

Golden Tree's fans and Kid's fandom had signed a temporary alliance pact, and their fan leaders were supposedly holding a summit too.

The reason for all that bait was to rally as much streaming firepower as possible.

And apparently it was going well.

"Manager Jang, haven't they been doing a ton of livestreams too?"

"Let me check… Hmm. Not that many, actually. But the view counts on each video are absolutely insane."

Both sides had massive fanbases spanning the entire globe.

The gap between their numbers and other artists' numbers was astronomical.

And that firepower was on full display across portal sites, community boards, and social media. An endless flood of posts, articles, and comments.

"At least the charts are still playing fair."

"Hm? I don't think so. It should've started today."

At my words, Manager Jang scrambled to check the streaming charts.

His jaw dropped and a hollow laugh escaped.

"Well, look at that. It already started."

Before promotions even began, the fans were syncing up their efforts. Warming up. Signaling to the public that the comeback was here.

They were streaming both artists' previous title tracks.

Kid and Golden Tree, effortlessly claiming the top spots on the charts.

The result of two massive fandoms joining forces.

"The album review section is probably a war zone right now."

"They'll be fighting in there for hours, I bet."

Manager Jang shook his head slowly.

"That Kid. When he was ours, he was the most reliable thing in the world. But thinking of him as competition? Sends a chill down my spine."

I let out a small laugh.

"Still, one Golden Tree member is on our team. Hyun-woo."

Beyond Hyun-woo, top-tier artists like R&B king Jung-ho Yoon and Crescendo had all participated in Yumi's album.

Every recording came back at the highest quality.

A genuine masterpiece had been made.

Manager Jang clicked his tongue.

"Even with Hyun-woo and the others, it's Yumi's album. Those artists aren't the ones promoting. Hyun-woo isn't doing music shows or performing on stage. They'll lend some firepower, sure, but M&K is going to pull way more attention. What we have is participation. What they have is a collaboration."

He was right.

That was exactly why we hadn't gone heavy on teasers from the start.

Out of sixteen brilliant tracks, we'd only shot music videos for the two title songs.

Double title.

I'd wanted to do all sixteen, but that wasn't happening.

"And the timing is garbage, too. Of all the times for this to overlap with Yumi's promotions. You think Director Park planned this to screw us over?"

Manager Jang narrowed his eyes with what I'd call reasonable suspicion.

"Manager Jang, whether Director Park targeted this timing or not doesn't matter. We can't adjust our schedule. This window is the best we've got."

We'd calculated the release date to align with the *Yumi's F.M.P.* broadcast schedule. The goal was a date where the maximum number of people wouldn't have to wait too long, where anticipation would be at just the right level for them to listen in a good mood.

Make them wait too long and interest fades. Put it out too early and the anticipation hasn't built enough.

"We go at our own pace. First place on the charts might be tough, but we can still burn ourselves into the public's memory."

Manager Jang smiled, a little bitter.

"Yeah. That's enough."

We were making a very foolish miscalculation at the time.

***

*A monster rookie that debuted two weeks ago and is sweeping through the music scene.*

*Yes, they've been incredibly hot lately, haven't they?*

*Eight in You!*

They'd aired a reality show on cable, and even before their debut they'd appeared on popular, high-rated variety shows.

Cute, pretty girls. Eight in You.

People called them the monster rookie, and they were growing at a pace that lived up to the title.

"They're good. That one's really improved her dancing."

At Jin-ah's comment, Yoon-jung nodded.

"She's getting the details right, too. But that one's still rough. She was the one who was good at rap, right?"

The members were watching the music broadcast from the dorm.

On screen, Eight in You performed with a fresh, cute concept, doing adorable, easy-to-follow choreography.

The song was catchy and simple, lodging itself right in your ear. They had the full trifecta for a rookie girl group trying to build popularity.

They hadn't done it this way before my regression.

*They weren't pushed this hard either. And it wasn't a song like this.*

Even so, they'd been popular.

Seven in You, without Hyun-hee Lee.

As a girl group from major agency HS Entertainment, they'd naturally drawn attention. They grew steadily, slowly, until one day something clicked and they blew up into a national girl group.

*They're better now.*

Their stage was sharper than it had been at debut in the original timeline.

*I changed the future.*

Girls who were supposed to become a national girl group over a slow burn were now growing at an accelerated pace.

*But then again… they only became the national girl group because our girls failed.*

The future I'd changed wasn't just theirs.

In a few hours, a song would drop.

The acoustic track Yumi and Hae-yeol had made together. "Like a Star."

Before releasing the full album, Yumi would kick off her first solo promotions with this single.

***

"You've been up since dawn getting ready. Tired? It's like that the first time. Give it a couple weeks and your body clock adjusts."

"Weirdly, I'm not tired at all."

"Ha! That's just nerves and excitement."

"Really?"

She grinned.

Yumi had filmed a solo variety show on public broadcast, and still here she was, nervous and excited for her first music show.

It had to feel different.

She wasn't a TV personality. She was a singer, and she'd been waiting for this moment for a long time.

We'd stopped by the salon at dawn and headed to the music show's studio for the pre-recording.

Current time: four in the morning.

The sky was steeped in deep blue.

Fans waited outside the studio. Beside them, people clustered in the 24-hour café and the nearby shops.

Scattered among them were foreigners, and women in hijabs.

They were either today's live audience or people who'd come just to catch a glimpse on their way to work.

Our van passed them and pulled into the parking lot behind the building.

A senior idol smoking by the back entrance widened his eyes when he saw us.

"Hello. I'm Yumi from Blue Sherbet."

He stubbed out his half-finished cigarette without hesitation and came toward Yumi with a broad smile.

Keeping a polite three steps of distance so the smoke wouldn't reach her, he greeted her in a warm voice.

"Hey. You're doing the music show today, right? I listened to 'Like a Star' as soon as it came out. Really loved it."

"Oh. Thank you, sunbae!"

"Good luck on the show today! I'll be rooting for you!"

Through the back entrance and inside, a woman emerging from the bathroom spotted Yumi and stopped dead in her tracks.

Then she scurried into the first-floor common waiting room.

We climbed the stairs, and Yumi greeted every single person she passed.

"Oh. Hello."

"Yes, yes. Hello."

"Congrats on your first show."

Staff greeted her back.

Not everyone was warm about it, but still. What a difference.

Normally, most idols would get a passing glance at best, maybe a brief nod. The fact that people were actually speaking to her meant they recognized her. Acknowledged what she'd done.

Some of those staff members who greeted her with an air of casual indifference were probably loyal viewers of her variety show.

That's how broadcast stations worked. There was an unspoken rule about not showing excitement around celebrities, and if you let it slip that you were a fan, you risked getting laughed at by your colleagues.

Third floor. The door to Yumi's assigned dressing room had her name on it alongside a senior idol group's name.

Getting a solo dressing room required seniority.

Nothing to be done about that.

Being assigned a shared room up here instead of the first-floor common area was already the production team doing their best for her first show.

"Hello. I'm Yumi from Blue Sherbet."

Magic Stage. TKK Entertainment's four-year girl group.

One sat up from where she'd been lying down. Another paused mid-makeup. They each took a step toward Yumi.

"Hey! 'Like a Star' was great. This is your first music show, right?"

"Yes, it's my first time. I really love your music too, sunbae."

The corners of their mouths twitched upward.

"Really?!"

"Yes, yes. Even as a trainee, I practiced to your songs a lot. I listened to your new one as soon as it dropped, too."

Most juniors said things like this to their seniors.

Even if it was true, it was the kind of thing everyone said.

But looking at their faces, a certain warmth had already taken root.

"Do you like jelly?"

Yumi's head whipped around.

One of the seniors held out a bag of jelly, arm extended.

Yumi padded over and bowed at the waist.

"Thank you!"

"Have you tried this kind?"

"Yes. It's one of my favorites."

"Sit here."

Lured in by jelly, naturally and completely.

Yumi settled into the sofa, nestled between them, chattering away.

Laughter blooming everywhere.

*You've got to be doing well first. Then people are nice.*

And on top of that, she was a composer. Someone who might send opportunities their way.

Even if the kindness was calculated, it was far better than nothing.

***

*Yumi's F.M.P.* had already hit ten percent in viewership ratings.

The show had extended beyond its originally planned run, now continuing with no set end date.

It would probably wrap only after airing the full response to the album release.

At this point, it was essentially a solo reality show for Yumi on public broadcast.

"We could've started filming earlier, you know."

PD Park sounded mildly aggrieved.

I smiled faintly and answered.

"We did get the footage of her going up on stage briefly during the live broadcast, PD."

Even artists who'd already pre-recorded would go on stage for a minute or two for the fans who'd shown up for the live taping.

Quick up, quick down. A minute, two at most.

"At least we salvaged that much."

I'd deliberately kept the film crew from coming earlier.

Another team shared the dressing room with Yumi. And more importantly, I wanted her to rest comfortably during the wait.

The last thing she needed was cameras amplifying her nerves.

Her first music show should be as stress-free as possible.

"Did you see the SNS posts, sir? Tons of verification posts going up."

"I saw. I'll need to send thank-you messages later."

The constellation of stars who'd worked on Yumi's album.

They'd posted photos on social media of themselves watching Yumi's first music show stage.

Messages of support.

And promotion to their own fanbases at the same time.

Just then, performers filed onto the stage one after another.

The first-place candidates stood at the front, waiting for the announcement.

We watched Yumi's face among them.

Lips pressed tight together. Tension radiating off her.

Her shoulders and neck were rigid enough to see from here.

"If she takes first place, that's the perfect picture. But with the monster rookie in the running, it might be tough, right, sir?"

PD Park asked with a sly expression.

His face said he was sure of first place.

By "monster rookie," he meant Eight in You.

But if they were the monster rookie, what kind of rookie would you call our Yumi?

"Eight in You is formidable. The push behind them is strong, and the fandom is growing fast. Since it's Yumi's first week on music shows, she might fall a little short on some metrics."

Of course. Obviously.

I was just being modest.

The song had hit number two on the streaming charts on release day and was still sitting at number five days later.

Not exactly a threat.

"Ha! Looks like your prediction's going to be wrong again, sir. You said the charts wouldn't favor her, but the response has been amazing."

One thing I could say for certain.

Separate from the quality of the song, this was broadcast momentum doing its work.

But that didn't make it unfair.

Every bit of that "broadcast momentum" was something Yumi had earned through her own ability.

Landing a solo variety show. Being loved by audiences of every age.

All of it was fruit she'd picked with her own hands.

"This week's number one is… Congratulations! Yumi Cho, 'Like a Star.'"

Including a first-place win by this overwhelming margin.

"We have to hear your thoughts! Ms. Yumi Cho?"

Yumi received the microphone from the MC, who delivered his lines like he was reading from a textbook.

The hand holding the mic trembled visibly.

Her eyes, which should have been facing the camera, were turned toward us instead.

Looking at me, tears pooling in her gaze.

"Pre… President. First place… I… *sniff*."

Oh, come on.

Cry if you want, but at least finish your sentence.

Jin-ah was going to give her so much grief for this.

"Thank you… truly."

First place on the music show.

Worth celebrating, sure. But this was only the starting line.

*The real weapons haven't even been drawn yet.*

Yumi's rise was far from over.
