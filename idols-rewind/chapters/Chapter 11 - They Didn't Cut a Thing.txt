Chapter 11 - They Didn't Cut a Thing?

There was a gap between the taping and the air date. Tuesday for recording, Friday for broadcast. In between, we made the rounds of various YouTube channels shooting live performance videos. The single was already out, and the MV was up on Hee-yeon's personal channel. Once the broadcast dropped, the live videos would follow one by one. The view count on the MV was nothing to write home about yet, but the real fight started now. We weren't in a hurry. We'd decided to wait. Patiently, unhurriedly. For the moment it exploded. That was a luxury we could afford because of how well *Yeol's Music Notebook* had gone.

Yoon-jung had been hyping the broadcast on her personal stream since Thursday, and she kept it up today. She had a natural stake in it — being an active idol was part of what drew viewers to her in the first place, so a group member appearing on TV was good for her too. Her audience was into it. If she'd started streaming after already making it big, none of this would exist, but since she was barely a step removed from being completely unknown, her viewers were watching the story unfold in real time.

"Boss! Something's wrong with Hee-yeon! She's eating a sausage with the wrapper still on!"

"Ah."

Hee-yeon pulled the sausage from her mouth — she'd been quietly chewing through the plastic without realizing it. The broadcast was minutes away. Her nerves had apparently reached their absolute limit.

"I keep telling you, the taping went well. There's nothing to worry about."

"Boss! Did my sister actually do well?"

Jin-ah. She knew Hee-yeon's personality too well to take that at face value. Of course she didn't believe it.

"Just watch. Hey, it's starting."

The members and I settled around the TV. When the broadcast began, every one of us went quiet at once. Silence fell over the room.

"Time to welcome our guest! Up first today — Hee-yeon Lee of the rookie girl group Blue Sherbet, performing 'Wish Upon A Moon!'"

Hee-yeon walked out to the center of the stage. The camera closed in on her motionless figure, then cut to the audience's confused faces. Building the drama deliberately. The next shot caught her raising her head and beginning to sing, then swept across the crowd as they came around.

"That's our unnie..."

"Incredible. Seriously, how is she this good?"

"Unnie! You were amazing!"

The members piled on, voices bright with pride. Meanwhile, Hee-yeon's face had been going steadily paler. The performance was over. The interview was next. The interview she remembered nothing about.

"Hello. I'm Hae-chun Lee, the greatest singer."

"...!"

"...?"

"...!"

The members completely lost it — doubled over, tears streaking down their faces. Watching them, I let out a long, slow breath.

*Thank goodness.*

The broadcast was landing just as well as the taping had. I watched with quiet satisfaction. Then, gradually, that satisfaction started giving way to something else.

*Hm?*

Twenty minutes gone. And Hee-yeon was still on screen.

"What's going on... they didn't cut any of it?"

Almost the entire taping had made it to air — the cover performance, the back-and-forth with Hae-yeol at the piano, everything. This was an unexpected bonus.

***

It was only natural. Reporters weren't going to let a story this good walk past them.

*Blue Sherbet's Hee-yeon Lee Stuns on Yeol's Music Notebook!*
*Who Is Hee-yeon Lee? Trending #1!*
*Even Hae-yeol Yoo Is Smitten — "Want to collab?"*
*Quirky Charm and a New Musical Genius — Has She Arrived?*

Her name started climbing the moment the interview aired, and by around midnight it had settled in at number one and held through the early hours. Nothing else major was happening that night, and it was late. Those were the conditions. Trending number one doesn't hit the way it used to, but for us it was exactly the kind of welcome sign we needed.

Clicking through to the search results didn't give you much to work with, though. Everything that came up was just coverage from the broadcast itself. Hee-yeon barely had more out there than a complete unknown. Some curious viewers stopped there. Others made their way to the MV. The view count moved noticeably with every refresh, and the comments and likes were showing real, meaningful growth.

*Came here from the broadcast — what is the MV quality though...??*
*The visual quality IS the MV quality, that's what I'm saying! The umbrella shot is an actual legend. Timestamp 1:32. I thought she was an actress.*
*Well, I'm done. Got me good. Ugh. This is going to be exhausting for a while.*
*The song is genuinely incredible. No wonder Hae-yeol Yoo was into it. Fully agreed.*
*lol hey Yoon-jung, get your unnie on a show sometime!! ^^ Why were you hiding her??*

The show didn't pull huge ratings on the live broadcast. But clips of Hee-yeon's appearance were spreading across the internet at a frightening speed. They were funny enough, striking enough, strange enough. That's what drove the pace. The members and I watched the reactions build and could barely keep the glee off our faces.

"Oppa, we made it!"

"Hey! Choi Jin-ah!"

"Let's be happy now, Oppa!"

"You're calling your boss Oppa!"

While we were all still buzzing, my phone vibrated.

LOAN.

*Boss. I can't thank you enough. If it weren't for you, I never would have found my talent. Not in my whole life. I'll never forget this. I mean every word.*

"Ah."

I needed to move on his independence. Fast. With Hae-yeol's comments putting a spotlight on the composer, everyone in this industry was going to be scrambling for LOAN.

Messages from Jong-woo and Eun-chul followed right after, one then the other.

*I've decided I'm going all-in on music videos from here on out. Finding my direction is entirely thanks to you. Thank you for trusting someone as inexperienced as me with this. Rooting for Hee-yeon, Blue Sherbet, and K Management! Fighting!*

*Really glad the MV is getting this response. I was lacking in a lot of ways, but I gave it everything I had. If I ever grow enough to make commercial films one day, I'd love to work with Hee-yeon then. I'll push myself to grow to her level. I hope we keep working together.*

I gave my head a small shake. These people would have gotten here on their own eventually. I wrote back to each of them carefully, then went over to Hee-yeon, who was sitting in a daze staring at the comments.

"Hee-yeon. Today's the last day you look at these. The negative comments are coming. Stay away from the comments section for a while."

"...Yes."

She was still processing it. One broadcast, and all that quiet had cracked open. Hard not to feel like the ground had shifted.

"Boss! What's next for my sister?"

Everyone looked to me at Jin-ah's question. I shrugged.

What else.

"Events."

"Oh! Events!"

"Yes!"

"..."

"Events? For real?"

This one had gone well for a lot of reasons, and the fact that the interview leaned heavy on music had let her quirky side shine without putting her on actual variety terrain. That same character was going to bring in a flood of variety offers going forward. All of them would get turned down. What was right for Hee-yeon was keeping variety to a minimum and building up her artistic depth over time. That moment had already played out once. It wasn't going to land the same way a second time. The math still hadn't changed: variety would cost her more than it gave.

***

The next day, Director Park of HS Entertainment read the headlines from his office and let out a quiet laugh. He'd already listened to the song. Already watched the MV.

"Well. Of course he could do this."

The man had been exactly the talent he'd wanted to keep. The talent he'd needed to reach the board. But that was done now. Hyuk had walked out and built his own thing elsewhere, and with him went the board directorship that had almost been in Director Park's hands.

He closed the browser window with cold eyes.

"Enjoy it while you can."

Falling from this height wouldn't even hurt yet. You had to wait until someone was at their absolute peak before you knocked them off — that was when the fall made the world go dark. He knew what that felt like.

"For now... I suppose a welcome-back greeting is in order."

LOAN. The composer who'd gone from nothing to blue-chip in a single day. Pulling him in would be a light opening jab.

"The harder things get, the more a person wants the security of somewhere big."

Director Park was a hundred percent certain LOAN would come.

***

LOAN couldn't collect himself. His song. People were loving his song like this. He'd been awake since the broadcast the night before, and it was nearly noon. He couldn't stop watching the responses come in. Even the occasional English comment, or something in another language entirely — he was running each one through a translator, wondering what foreign listeners were hearing when they heard his music.

"This can't be real... seriously... this can't be real..."

*Honestly, the more you watch this, the more insane the visuals are? Looks low-budget but what IS this.*
*Shut up, everyone. Everything in this video is just backdrop for that yellow dress. She's being shy, right? Unnie, why would you do this to me. That expression on that face. What am I supposed to do with that...*
**WARNING:** *This video may be hazardous to your heart.*
**WARNING:** *This video may alter your identity. Fans of other groups: your home base is at risk.*
*Excuse me?? Is nobody going to mention that the song is absolutely incredible??*
└ *That part's already established fact. Everyone knows.*

The response wasn't cooling — if anything, it was running hotter with every hour. Praise for the video, praise for Hee-yeon's looks and style and voice, praise for the music. All of it flooding in.

"..."

And on top of that, his SoundCloud inbox was fattening up. Hyuk had told him to take down his contact details, so everything was going to messages, and the messages kept coming.

One stood out.

*Hello, composer LOAN. This is HS Entertainment.*

The contents were something else. An offer to join the HS Entertainment publishing team. The distance he'd traveled hit him all at once. That this kind of message, from HS Entertainment, was sitting in his inbox.

"Wow. Even HS is coming for me now."

LOAN marveled at it as he deleted every message in the folder. The one from HS included. Being impressed was one thing. Actually going somewhere was another. In his head, there was only one company. There wasn't even a space where another option could exist. If anything, the HS offer just made him more grateful to Hyuk, the person who'd made all of this happen. If Director Park ever found out, the man might actually stand there with his jaw on the floor.

LOAN read Hyuk's reply one more time. A promise to help him go independent.

"Hurry up and make money, then come get me, Boss."

It was the most LOAN thing he could have said. Before Hyuk's regression, HS Entertainment had been the one to raise him up into the light. To them he had given everything, right to the end.

He kept watching the responses with bleary eyes. Then a comment appeared that made him blink and look again.

*Congrats on #100.*

"What...?"

***

"One hun — one hun — a hundred! It entered the charts! It's at a hundred!"

Jung-han's eyes went wide as saucers. Both arms flew up.

*Finally.*

A slow grin spread across my face. Once a song broke into the top hundred, it had wind in its sails. Didn't matter anymore who'd missed the clips or missed the broadcast. Our song was going to start playing at bars, restaurants, supermarkets, cafes — it would be everywhere.

"Boss! Shouldn't we be doing something?!"

"We're fine. Just sit tight. The chart will climb on its own for a while."

I wasn't worried about it dropping. This song had that kind of force to it.

"We don't have to row. The public will do it for us."
