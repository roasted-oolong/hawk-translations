# Idols Rewind — Chapter 3: The Avengers Plan

Jin-ah's session ran until nine in the morning.

Not that they were done. They still had to do it all again with the rest of the group.

"Ugh. I'm dead. I'm starving."

"We need to pick up the others anyway. Let's go to the dorm, eat, and head back out."

"Okay."

Jin-ah dropped into the passenger seat of the Starex, soaked through.

She smelled. But he wasn't going to make a face at that, she'd sweated through a full session. Hard to hold that against someone.

...She'd better wash today, though. If she showed up smelling like this again tomorrow, he'd have every right to react.

He started the engine. Jin-ah connected her phone via Bluetooth and put on Beyoncé's "If I Were A Boy."

After drilling it for hours, she was still listening. She really did put in the work. And the talent was there to go with it.

"...Boss."

"Yeah?"

About a minute in, Jin-ah was pinching her nose and glaring at him sideways.

"Can you get an air freshener or something for this car?"

"..."

Has it occurred to you that might be coming from you?

He couldn't say it.

"Let's just open the windows. Bear with it for today."

"Ugh. Seriously."

"..."

The dorm came up fast.

It had been a while. This beat-up old place.

He climbed the villa stairs with a strange, layered feeling in his chest.

'Why am I so nervous.'

He didn't know what face to make when he saw everyone. He might actually cry — at the thought that he had a real shot at fixing everything he'd gotten wrong.

'Don't. They'll think something is seriously wrong.'

Keep it together. Act normal.

Jin-ah shoved through the door and went straight in.

He followed.

"Hey! What is everyone doing in there! Get up! We have practice! I've been at it for hours already!"

She'd put in real work this morning — but she'd originally shown up because she was bored, and now she was making the most of it. Hard to see what bragging to people who were still asleep was going to accomplish.

'Honestly.'

The nerves broke apart all at once.

He stood in the middle of the living room and took it in.

Familiar. And then, a voice even more familiar than the room.

"Boss. You're here?"

He'd heard a voice close to this one just a few hours ago — but it hit completely differently now.

"...Yeah. Hee-yeon. Sleep okay?"

Hee-yeon came shuffling out, arms stretching wide overhead, still not quite awake.

Without meaning to, he layered this image over the Hee-yeon he'd seen last night — the one before the regression. She'd carried herself a little differently then. Or maybe it was just the morning softening things.

"Yes. Slept great. Did you get any rest, Boss?"

"Yeah, I did. We need to eat and get to practice, so start getting ready."

"Okay. I'll go wash up."

She slipped into the bathroom. From Yoon-jung and Yumi's room, Jin-ah's voice broke through.

"What are you two doing! We need to leave for practice!"

"Hee-yeon unnie went to wash up. Go clean yourself off first. You stink."

"I stink because I've been practicing. Yoon-jung Hwang. Get up and start stretching at least."

"Oh come on."

"The Boss is here."

"...What?"

He stood watching the door from the living room. Yoon-jung pushed it open and came out.

Tall and lean, large eyes. She stood alongside Hee-yeon as the group's visual — not one thing about her fell short of a runway. Their main dancer.

She spotted him, bent at the waist, and greeted him quietly.

"You're here, Boss."

"Yeah. Sleep okay?"

"Um, yeah..."

She trailed off, eyes sliding away.

"Been busy gaming?"

"Uh... well... um... yes..."

"Good. Keep it up whenever you can. Don't worry about what anyone thinks."

"...What?"

"I'm saying aim for ranked while you're at it. It'll pay off someday."

He still couldn't quite believe it — after the company fell apart, she'd taken off on YouTube of all places. Gaming, song covers, dance covers, original choreography — a bottomless well, content that never ran dry. A model-stunning beauty who danced at an elite level, sang well, and could hold her own at a serious gaming rank. That combination pulled in fans from overseas and got her to ten million subscribers. More than half came through the dance content, but plenty of people who showed up for the gaming never left once they found the dancing.

She had an idol background and a clean public image, so TV appearances came easily. She ended up on the judging panel for audition shows as a dance evaluator.

"Hey! Get up already! How is the youngest one the most useless?"

"Ugh, unnie! I was already awake. How is anyone supposed to stay asleep when you're screaming like that?"

"You're supposed to come out and greet the Boss. You think I woke you up for fun?"

"Okay, okay. Relax."

Out came the youngest member, Yumi Cho.

Round-faced and bright-eyed — that openly charming, magnetic kind of cute that fans completely fell apart over. She bowed her head.

"Boss. Have you eaten?"

"Waiting to eat with everyone."

His eye caught her hand as they talked. A bandaged finger.

She noticed, and gave a small, slightly embarrassed smile.

"I was changing a guitar string and slipped... sorry."

"Nothing to be sorry about. Lucky it didn't happen during the promotion period. Not too serious?"

"No, it's fine. Should be healed up soon."

Yumi was the group's sub-vocalist — the easy target, the one who took the hits. Jin-ah's go-to.

"Still working on music?"

"A little... I have some things put together, but they're not very good."

And her biggest talent. Composing and writing.

Ballads, dance tracks, rock, hip-hop, EDM. The biggest star in the group would be Hee-yeon, but Yumi had probably made the most money of any of them. How everything she touched managed to succeed was almost baffling.

Her talent wasn't fully in bloom yet — same as Jin-ah earlier. But that wasn't fixed. With the right push, she might open up faster than she had the first time.

'Still gets me every time.'

His own eye for talent was almost unsettling. One group — this lineup.

'No wonder people called it a miracle company. Hard to argue with the mockery.'

From here on out, they'd be saying it very differently.

***

They put together a quick salad breakfast and headed to the practice room.

The office and dorm were close — ten minutes on foot. The practice room meant taking transport. He'd been sure he'd be generating income fast enough to upgrade everything: the setup, the office, the practice space. He hadn't. So the inconvenience stayed.

Once they arrived and the members began stretching, he spoke.

"Today we're doing individual skills checks."

Startled faces all around — except for Jin-ah, who was already grinning.

"I already went this morning. Just do what you normally do. Why is everyone freaking out?"

Nobody listened to her.

He looked at Hee-yeon.

"Let's start with you."

Their main vocalist. The artist the public would fall completely in love with. A gifted actress on top of that. A terrifying depth of talent that had come to people's attention late, and then just kept growing with no ceiling anywhere in sight.

He wanted to hear her now. Where had she been at this point?

"Should I start with singing?"

"Go ahead. Your choice."

She warmed up briefly, then pulled out her phone and connected to the practice room speaker.

The opening notes came through.

He laughed, quiet and involuntary.

Across the room, he caught Jin-ah's eyes going wide.

It tracked, actually. A skills check meant showing range. She had chosen a song that would let her put everything on the table — technique, expression, all of it.

A song every serious trainee had drilled a thousand times, and that almost none of them had ever really done justice.

Beyoncé's "Halo."

Drums, piano, strings, and a clap track, and then the voice came in.

"Remember those walls I built."

Eyes closed, body moving lightly with the rhythm. From the very first note, her voice started settling into his chest.

That kind of pull from the opening.

The song left nowhere to hide. Because of how impossibly skilled Beyoncé was, every single measure exposed a singer's exact standing — power, technique, emotion, range, fundamentals, personality, color, ad-libs. Every single one.

That was why he was watching with his mouth slightly open.

'She was already... nearly there.'

Her talent had bloomed almost to its full form. The song built toward its peak and before it even arrived she'd already shown her head voice, vibrato, breath control, and dynamic range.

"I can't feel your halo, halo. I can't see your halo, halo."

No microphone, and the room rang with the weight of it. And the depth of feeling behind every word.

It came back to him now.

He'd known exactly what she was capable of — and he'd kept it to himself, convinced it was better to let the group rise as a unit first.

'What a waste.'

He'd told himself he could show this side of her later. That the group coming up first would make it land harder. That there was time.

...He knew better now. All four of these members could command their own spotlight. Not all at once, not in the same way. But not one of them needed to wait in someone else's shadow.

The song ended. He smiled and gave a slow nod.

"Really good. You know there's still a lot further you can go, right?"

"Me?"

She looked genuinely surprised. She couldn't see her own ceiling — or more accurately, the fact that she didn't have one.

"Yeah. A lot further."

"...I'll work at it."

"Okay. Let's see your dancing now."

"Sure."

The dancing started, and something came back to him that he'd almost forgotten. The group had a very particular kind of balance.

Hee-yeon could not dance. It was the complete inverse of her voice.

'...Right. Warm, hardworking, great actress, gorgeous, one of the best vocalists he'd ever heard — and just genuinely cannot dance. That's her human side.'

He finished the individual checks on all four of them and made his announcement.

"We're suspending group activities for now."

Stunned faces, all four of them.

"And starting individual activities."

"...What?"

"Jin-ah. You're going to stay focused on dance and vocal training going forward. What you've already been doing — just keep that up. And Yumi. From here on you're going all in on composing and writing. Arrangements especially. Arrange everything you can get your hands on. I was about to say 'you mentioned you'd feel something click' — anyway. Something will click. You'll feel it. And you already know you need to keep your singing and dancing from going stale, right?"

The members had figured out they'd jumped to the wrong conclusion, and they settled in to actually listen.

"Yoon-jung. Keep your voice in shape, but your focus for now is dance and gaming. And Hee-yeon—"

"Yes? Yes?"

Yoon-jung went blank.

He gave a small shrug like the answer was obvious.

"You're going to start on YouTube and streaming. For dance, cover choreography and original work — not basic group choreo, but stuff that actually shows your level. And get your gaming up to where you can put it on stream. Once you're there, we start."

He turned from the still-stunned Yoon-jung and looked at Hee-yeon.

"You're starting activities now. A solo track. Can you do it?"

"..."

A few seconds of quiet settled over the practice room. Then Hee-yeon answered.

"I'll do it. I trust you, Boss."

"Good. Nobody worry. Just trust me. I'm getting all of you to the top."

He could already hear exactly what the industry would say. The group flopped, so he's pouring everything into one person and planning to use her name to package the rest of them in behind her. That was going to be the read.

Instead of the Power Rangers model, where the team only works when everyone's together, he needed to go Avengers. Each one a force on her own, and when they came together, something bigger than what the numbers should add up to.

Toxic solo fandoms? If all four built their own massive followings independently, those fans wouldn't be at each other's throats — they'd be counting down. They'd know their favorite wasn't being held back, that the group only made everyone stronger. The anticipation alone would be its own kind of momentum.

'...Honestly, letting them think that might not be a bad thing.'

Lower their guard. Keep them underestimating.

'First thing, then... I need to find LOAN.'

A pearl still buried in the mud right now. This time, he was going to be the one who dug it out.
