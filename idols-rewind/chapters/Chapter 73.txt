# Monster Rookie from Korea

HS Entertainment, Team 1.

Team Lead Kim's voice erupted in a roar.

"We need to do *something*! How does it make any sense that we're losing to some boring little acoustic song? Hae-yeol Yoo? How long ago was he even relevant?"

Veins bulging in his neck.

Face flushed red.

The manager in charge of Eight in You already had cold sweat soaking through the back of his shirt.

Still, he tried to answer as calmly as he could. He knew that simply bowing his head and apologizing wouldn't make this go away. An apology would only earn him a beating.

"The problem isn't Hae-yeol Yoo. We shot our reality show on cable, but they had a weekend primetime slot on public broadcast. The promotional effect on the song is worlds apart. Ever since Yumi revealed her identity, the buzz has been building nonstop, and she's accumulated a thick layer of fans on top of it. Even if some of that is foam, it adds up."

Stifled gasps from around the room. A vein pulsed on Team Lead Kim's forehead.

The manager had expected this atmosphere. His lips went pale, but he pressed on to the end.

"The numbers Eight in You is pulling right now are actually well above what most successful rookies manage. The outlook is bright. The fandom is growing fast, name recognition is climbing. By the next comeback, we should see a major jump."

"...So what you're saying is, we already lost this round, let's just give up?"

"..."

"Is that supposed to be an answer!?"

The manager squeezed his eyes shut.

This had been a scolding from the start. There was never going to be a right answer.

Managers from Teams 2 and 3 had their ears perked toward the noise pouring out of Team 1's office, looking for something to chew on.

Popcorn, for instance.

One person who'd been about to step out for a cigarette held back and gulped water instead.

But soon enough, they started slipping away one by one.

Director Park had come stalking in with an irritable stride.

"Team Lead Kim."

"Yes, sir..."

"Was the push not enough?"

"...I'm sorry."

The demon who'd been tearing into his manager moments ago was nowhere to be found. Team Lead Kim had become a mouse before a cat.

Director Park's voice cracked like a whip.

"Sorry doesn't cut it! It wasn't even a full album. You lost to some flimsy acoustic track!"

The truth was, Director Park knew.

There was nothing anyone could have done.

The public attention on Yumi had been enormous. The senior artists scrambling over each other on social media to participate in her album had been the decisive blow.

Maybe that was why there was no real defeat on his face.

At the end of the day, it was still just the debut single of a freshly launched girl group.

For a debut, the numbers weren't bad.

And the potential for significant growth over time was clearly there.

*The real weapon hasn't even been drawn yet.*

This girl group was a stepping stone for the future, nothing more.

The real weapon was the Kid and Min-seok collab.

*A full album?*

Director Park was confident. The collab might fall slightly short in artistic completeness, but in raw fandom firepower, M&K would be overwhelmingly superior.

"Set up a meeting to prepare for the Japan activities."

"...Sir? What about the follow-up single?"

"Get it ready so we can go straight into it as soon as Japan wraps."

Overseas, they would secure fans before Blue Sherbet could.

**

Two consecutive weeks at number one on the music shows.

We swept up every trophy available.

But even that was starting to lose steam.

The streaming chart position had settled around tenth.

Not something to be disappointed about, really. The climb had been powered by broadcast momentum, and regardless of how good the song was, it wasn't the kind of track built to hold its ground on the charts.

*A song like this has its own kind of influence.*

I was looking right at it.

The streets of Hongdae.

A guy strumming an acoustic guitar was singing the melody of "Like a Star."

"Seriously, this song is so good. My mom is obsessed with it. She changed her ringtone *and* her caller tune to this."

"I like listening to this on my commute home. Feels like it gives you a hug, you know?"

Voices drifting from the crowd that had gathered.

"Wanna hit up the coin karaoke? I've been practicing this one."

"Wait, me too. Duet?"

"Someone's busking this song over here too?"

"It's got that sentimental thing going on, you know? Look at how many people are stopping."

That was the kind of pull Yumi's song had.

Falling away from the charts, but settling deeper into daily life.

Karaoke popularity rankings were climbing. Buskers were playing it everywhere. Cover videos were sprouting up left and right. The song was gradually slipping from the top of the charts, yet somehow it felt more loved than ever.

I smiled to myself and turned around.

The stuffiness from the hat pulled low and the mask over my face vanished without a trace.

I grabbed the coffees and opened the van door.

"Thank you!"

"Boss, I could've gotten those. People recognize your face now. You didn't need to go yourself."

Yumi and Jung-han, taking their cups.

I pulled off the hat and mask and smoothed down my flattened hair.

"It's fine. You're driving. The least I can do is grab coffee. How much time do we have?"

"Almost an hour until the scheduled time."

When you're running between events all day, sometimes you're scrambling with no time to breathe, and sometimes you hit a gap like this where time just floats.

The next venue was close enough to reach in ten minutes. We'd planned to rest here for a bit.

"Did you hear my song just now? I think I caught a bit of it."

Bright, eager eyes looking up at me.

"Yeah. Someone was busking it."

"Really?"

She clapped once and beamed.

I smirked. "There are dozens of videos going up every day of people singing your song. Why the surprise?"

"I've never seen it with my own eyes before! How were people reacting?"

"Well, obviously they were—"

I was about to embellish what I'd seen and heard when a familiar intro drifted in from outside.

Our heads turned at the same time.

Not far from where we'd parked. One person on guitar, another holding a mic.

Two people just starting a busking set.

Yumi pressed herself flat against the window.

"Whoa!"

Passersby were slowing down. Some stopped entirely and drifted closer to watch.

"People are gathering!"

She was practically vibrating. Her finger pressed against the glass, pointing at the crowd.

She'd seen plenty of enthusiastic audiences at her own events. And yet here she was, like this.

Maybe there was a special thrill in watching it secretly from the outside. Seeing it from the observer's seat hit different.

Whatever it was, Yumi was happy, and that made me happy too.

As the busking duo's song neared its end, Yumi murmured under her breath.

"I want to sing so bad... It's not time yet, right?"

There was still a decent stretch before the event. Short if you thought of it one way, long if you thought of it another.

From the look on Yumi's face, it felt very, very long.

I quietly studied the scene outside. Their position, the distance to our van. I mapped it carefully.

And the answer arrived on its own.

"Want to do one song over there?"

"...!"

"...!"

Two pairs of eyes went wide as dinner plates.

"Really!? I can really do it? For real?"

Yumi was a professional. If she was going to perform, she deserved to be compensated for it.

And that was an open space with no security setup. Nothing had been prepared. From where I stood, thinking about the one-in-ten-thousand chance, I should have said no.

But.

Surprise performances happened all the time, both overseas and at home. More overseas than here, admittedly. Billboard superstars dropped into street buskers' sets out of nowhere. Others stumbled out at dawn, still buzzed, and played guitar on some quiet residential street.

"Go ahead. But just one song."

"I'll do it!"

She grabbed my arm like her life depended on it, face urgent, as if I might take it back.

"Boss... will it be safe enough?"

"The location's not bad. There's a wall behind them, and the ledges around the area naturally funnel where people sit. And you and I are both here. If anything happens, we grab her and bring her straight to the van."

There was a one-in-ten-thousand risk. But that was all it was. That same risk existed at any venue, any fan signing, any fan meet.

You don't skip making the soy paste just because you're scared of bugs.

"This kind of experience is worth paying for. Especially for Yumi."

Right now, her world was a loop between event venues, the dorm, the practice room, and music shows. This busking could be a new kind of stimulus. Even if it didn't produce immediate results, the experience would nourish something in her sensibility.

And right on cue.

"Looks like they just finished. I'll go talk to them. They might say no."

But that didn't happen. I pulled my mask down slightly, and fortunately they recognized me. When I asked if Yumi could sing one song, they nodded so hard their heads nearly came off.

"Yumi, let's go."

The van door slid open and Yumi stepped out.

"Ohhhhh!"

"Wait, wait!? Wait!"

"It's Yumi Cho!"

With Jung-han and me flanking her on both sides, nobody could get close. They just shouted her name.

A crowd materialized out of thin air, phones already raised and filming.

"I really enjoyed your performance. Thank you for singing my song."

"Oh, no, thank *you*. I mean! I'm a fan!"

"Would it be okay if I sang one song?"

They'd already given permission, but refusing now, in front of this crowd, would earn them enough curses to last several lifetimes.

"Do you need the guitar too?"

"Yes! Thank you!"

Yumi slung the guitar over her shoulder and plucked the strings, checking the tuning.

"Love your music! You're so cute!"

"You're gorgeous!"

Pink bloomed across both of Yumi's cheeks. The spontaneity of it all had her visibly excited. Her lips were already curved up to her ears, her eyes soft crescents.

"Is that Yumi Cho? Yumi Cho!?"

"That's Yumi Cho!"

Voices from farther away. The crowd that had already gathered like a cloud was apparently planning to grow even bigger.

At this rate, even if she wanted to do more, she couldn't. It might start causing problems for the shops around them.

Tuning confirmed, she leaned into the mic.

"Hi, everyone. I'm Yumi Cho from Blue Sherbet. I happened to be nearby, and these two were singing my song so beautifully that I suddenly wanted to sing too. I'm just going to do one song and go, so please keep things orderly. And after I leave, please stick around and enjoy their music."

A roar of cheers erupted.

"Okay. Here I go."

Yumi began to strum.

All that practice had paid off. The song was simple by design, but from the very first notes of the intro, people's faces softened.

Then her voice flowed out through the small speaker.

Vocals smooth as honey.

Smiles bloomed across the crowd.

They were sinking slowly into the feeling. Like her steady guitar and her voice were swallowing every other sound around them.

The noisy street went still.

Nothing but guitar and singing.

Across the way, people were pressed against the windows of the shops, staring. A worker in an apron stood frozen outside a store entrance.

Everyone had stopped what they were doing. Everyone was smiling. Everyone was listening.

"That was amazing! Go Yumi!"

"Your song is incredible!"

People waving, calling her name.

When the song ended, we made our way back to the van at an easy pace. Yumi climbed in and let out a long, flushed breath.

"That was the absolute best!"

Everyone out there probably felt the same way.

It really was the best.
