Idols Rewind — Chapter 1: The Super Manager's Regression

"Manager Kang! Get over here already! Why are you so late?"

"Sorry, Director. I stopped to pick up umbrellas."

He shook the rain from his shoulders and leaned three long umbrellas against the wall beside him.

A glance at the table told him the two of them had already put away two bottles of soju on their own. The halibut was half gone too.

"Is it raining out?"

"Just a little. I was on the phone with the kids when it started coming down, so I grabbed these from the place out front. One for you and one for Team Lead Na."

"Long umbrellas... I like it. Always preferred a long one over those little fold-up things. Same goes for a career — the longer the better."

The Director looked him dead in the eye.

Team Lead Na laughed quietly. "Director, that's not going to work. He handed in his resignation today."

"Oh? Well. You never know. Manager Kang. Ah — no, that's not right anymore. Hyuk Kang. So you're really going off to start your own company?"

He scratched the back of his head. Everyone kept asking him the same thing. Like they already knew the answer.

"Yeah. I want to give it a real shot."

"Makes sense. A talent like yours — who wouldn't want more? And that eye of yours, Manager Kang. That eye is something else. Add everything else you've got, and you're the only senior manager in this whole industry anyone would call a big shot. So. Think it over one more time."

Director Park finished with that easy, leading tone. Still trying to pull him back. He'd lost count of how many times.

'We already had a farewell party yesterday. Why are we doing this again?'

That was why today's send-off was only three people, unlike the packed room the night before. The Director knew he was close to Team Lead Na, so he'd used that to get Na in the door — a farewell party in name only.

Watching him refuse to quit, he thought again that the nickname "Pitbull" wasn't given for nothing. Once this man bit down, he never let go.

He glanced at Team Lead Na, a silent plea for help. The person he trusted most here. Someone he actually respected. No matter how much groundwork the Director had laid, Team Lead Na would be on his side.

But.

'He's playing dumb...?'

Was it because, in their eyes, he'd already left? Or did Team Lead Na actually want him to stay too?

Team Lead Na had caught the look and said nothing, lips pressed shut. Only the Director kept going.

"I'm telling you — I'll make you a team lead. I'll build Team 4 around you, set it up however you want. What's the difference between growing artists out there versus in here? It'll actually be easier here. You'll be able to do everything you've been itching to do. Nobody touches Team Lead Kang — I'll guarantee it personally. And that goes for me too. I won't touch anything you do. I mean that."

He was already Team Lead Kang. A minute ago it was Hyuk Kang.

Problem was, he was pretty sure he'd rather hear CEO. Because no matter how well he performed here, Director felt like a ceiling. Unless he somehow got Director Park bumped to executive first, team lead was where he'd stay.

"I'm sorry. I really do appreciate it, but I still want to go out on my own."

"...Oh?"

Director Park laughed, short and sharp, and started turning his glass in his hands. He reached quickly for the soju bottle to refill it.

Thud. A hand stopped him.

He looked up to find Director Park's face had gone cold, the bottle already lifted from his grip. The air dropped to freezing in an instant.

Director Park poured his own glass and knocked it back.

"Hah. Manager Kang. You know what they say? You can spot a great tree by its first leaves."

It wasn't really a question. He kept his mouth shut.

"And do you know 낭중지추? An awl in a sack."
*(T/N: 낭중지추 — literally "an awl in a sack." A classical Chinese idiom meaning true talent always makes itself known, no matter how it's contained.)*

The seedling and the awl. Both meant to be him.

"And... the nail that sticks up gets hammered down?"

"...!"

"Director!"

Director Park glanced at Team Lead Na, then turned back to him.

"You've put your name out there too much. Shown too much of what you're capable of. You think people are going to just let that go? Have you thought about what hyenas do to a lion cub that strays from the pride?"

Anyone listening could hear it was a warning now. Persuasion hadn't worked, so here came the threats.

He kept his voice level.

"A lion cub can handle anything that isn't its own mother or brothers. That's exactly why it leaves."

He'd meant it as a polite way to ask them to let him go cleanly.

"Even when its brothers said they needed their youngest? Even after they asked it not to go?"

Not a chance, that answer said.

Director Park's words hit him harder than he let on. A division head title at a major agency like HS Entertainment was no small thing — least of all for someone about to start from scratch on his own.

But if a threat like this was enough to make him fold, none of this would've happened in the first place. He'd have been sitting in that Team 4 office a long time ago.

He stood up without a word. Team Lead Na pressed both hands over his face and breathed out slow. There really wasn't a place left for him here anymore.

"Better to walk away than to keep tearing at each other inside the same fence."

"...!"

"I hope the day comes when we can help each other from our own fences."

Director Park's eyes were ice.

He held their gazes for a moment — the Director's, then Team Lead Na's — and bowed at a full ninety degrees before walking out.

He stepped outside with one of the long umbrellas. The rain was coming down hard.

"A lion, please." He walked through the rain with the corner of his mouth twisted up. "I was the only actual lion in that whole room of hyenas."

The next day, instead of taking artists whose contracts were almost up, he walked out with only four trainees. His own quiet way of saying goodbye on good terms. He could have taken every cash cow he wanted if he'd decided to.

***

"Haah."

He sat on the wooden platform on his rooftop room and looked up at the full moon.
*(T/N: 옥탑방 — a rooftop room, a small, cheap living space built on top of older buildings in Korea. A common symbol of financial hardship and starting from the bottom.)*

Three empty soju bottles sat beside him, one more half-finished, a snack bag ripped open and forgotten.

"Guess I was just another hyena. And here I thought I was the lion."

How had it come to this. Looking at his own life felt embarrassing.

Between B (Birth) and D (Death) there is C (Choice).

What would have happened if he'd just taken the Director up on that offer?

"At least I wouldn't be sitting here like this."

The moon kept shining exactly as bright. He had no light left in him at all.

Big-shot manager. Right. He'd gone from washed-up to full-on scrap, and there was nothing in between worth remembering.

What was the point of talent if it just got you crushed under someone else's foot?

"Oppa."

A voice out of nowhere.

He turned.

"...Hee-yeon? What are you doing here. How did you even find this place?"

His eyes must have been shaking — he could feel it.

There was a look and a presence that simply did not belong in a neighborhood like this. Not just this neighborhood — she was one of the biggest stars in the country.

"Mind if I join you?"

Hee-yeon Lee slipped off her shoes and stepped up onto the platform, sitting down properly with her back straight. It was strange. One of Korea's top stars, sitting in a place like this. And yet somehow, just by being there, she made the whole place feel less shabby.
*(T/N: Oppa — a term used by a younger woman to address an older male she is close to, whether a brother, close friend, or someone she looks up to. It carries warmth and familiarity.)*

"...What are you doing here."

"Is this somewhere I'm not supposed to come?"

She said it like she'd just seen him yesterday. Completely natural. It had been years.

"Yeah, it is. You have no business being in a place like this. What is a star like you doing here."

"I'm a little late, I know. I came to pay you back. I think I'm finally in a position to actually protect the people I care about. You ended up like this because you were trying to protect us, Oppa. And I missed you. No matter where I looked, I couldn't find a single person like you anywhere."

He let out a short laugh. He still felt like he owed them — and she was here talking about debts.

"What debt? The debt from me dragging you all out and barely getting you to success eventually?"

"I never saw it that way. What happened to us was their fault. You did everything you could."

"Results are what matter in this world."

"Maybe everyone else in this industry thinks that. But you're someone who cares about the how. I know that about you."

"And you don't think that's exactly why we failed?"

"No. You weren't wrong."

This stubborn girl. Some things really don't change no matter how many years go by.

He shook his head.

"Fine. You're right about all of it. But if you really want to pay me back — disappear right now and never come back. That's the payment. Watching you keep succeeding chips away at the guilt little by little."

Her expression didn't move. She picked up his glass, poured herself a drink, and set it back down.

"Let's start over. Stop letting your talent rot like this. Every person you ever believed in — look at where they are now. Every song, every project, everything you chose got recognized. You were like that even when we were trainees. You always seemed like you could see straight through to what mattered."

"If I could really see straight through everything, would I have ended up here?"

She wasn't wrong, though. His eye for songs, for projects, for people — it had always been different. Whether someone would catch on with the public, whether they'd burn bright and fade, whether they had real talent, whether their character was sound. He could usually tell without much effort. He'd assumed that meant success would follow naturally. He was wrong. A good eye didn't solve everything.

"Don't be so negative. You have something most people don't."

"I've been wrong plenty of times."

"That was about management, not instincts."

"Like how I couldn't manage you all to success, you mean?"

A short, humorless laugh. Did Hee-yeon see him as someone bitter and eaten up by it? He hoped so. He wanted her to look at him and think — this one's done — and walk away.

"I made it, but I'm not happy. I don't have anywhere to put what I feel. You're not there, and the others aren't there. I feel like I'm just... alone."

"That's what it looks like from the outside when someone has everything."

"Back then we were hungry. But at least we were all together."

"Everyone romanticizes the past. That's all that is. Put those same people back in that situation today and not one of them would go."

"What about you, Oppa? What about you?"

He laughed a little and looked around.

"Isn't that obvious? I can't think of a time things were worse than this. And for the record — I'm not romanticizing. This is just what it is."

"So then let's try again."

"Did you not hear what I just said? Walking away and never coming back — that's how you pay me back."

"I heard you. But you said you feel guilty about me. So pay that back instead. Get back out there and actually do something with what you have. That's how you settle it — not sitting in some corner watching me on TV and letting the guilt slowly wear away. That's not repayment. That's just making yourself feel okay."

She'd gotten a lot more words in her since he last saw her. Things change, things stay the same. That's time.

But his answer hadn't changed.

"Go home."

"No."

"Maybe if I could actually go back. But right now I have nothing to give you. You're already a top star. And I'm this."

"The guilt—"

"That's not going to work."

He looked at her straight on, quiet and steady. Her eyes moved, just slightly.

"Thank you. For thinking of me."

"...I didn't say I was giving up. I'll come back."

"Don't."

"I will."

She left without giving an inch. He wouldn't either. She had her life, and he had his. Too much had changed between them to keep holding onto the past, and too many years had gone by.

He drank what was left in long, fast gulps.

"Ugh—!"

Bitter. Way too bitter. It hadn't tasted like this just a little while ago.

He dropped back flat on the wooden platform. A bottle got kicked off and rolled somewhere below him, but his eyes went straight to the full moon and stayed there.

"Just making myself feel better?"

Wrong. There was no comfort in a life like this.

Even now, years later, the regret hadn't faded. He'd been so stubborn about doing things right. He should never have done it that way.

"I miss it too, Hee-yeon. If I could really go back to that time... I think I'd actually get it right."

If he could go back... he really believed he wouldn't fail.

His eyelids grew heavy, and he was gone.

And then the light of the full moon poured over Hyuk Kang's body — and shimmered, strange and bright, like something had just woken up.
