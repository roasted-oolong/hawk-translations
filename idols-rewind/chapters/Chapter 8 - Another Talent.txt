Chapter 8 - Another Talent

The day had finally come.

The plan to let each member shine in her own lane was well underway. Yoon-jung was already producing real results through her streams. But whatever else we were doing, we were still a talent agency. And today was about the work.

First recording session since the regression. I couldn't help feeling the weight of it.

I wasn't the only one.

For LOAN, this was his first proper studio session. For Hee-yeon, it was the first step toward a solo debut she was carrying entirely on her own shoulders. Both of them were strung tight.

*This isn't going to go well.*

I knew it. But I was just as nervous myself, so I was in no position to say a word.

As it turned out, the worry was wasted.

The moment recording started with the studio engineer, something shifted in both of them. I watched it happen in their eyes.

*Right. These two were geniuses.*

Only then did I let myself settle back and watch the work.

The song was "Wish Upon A Moon".

Hee-yeon's voice came through the speakers.

She sang like nerves were someone else's problem. Not a single wobble.

Wish Upon A Moon.
I made a wish to the moon.
There's no way a miracle will happen, but just in case.
It can't be, but just in case.

The song was bright, and it held onto the warm, rough feeling from that very first session between them.

Hee-yeon's languid voice settled over something crisp and cozy and polished.

She bounced through the rhythm with that gorgeous vocal like it cost her nothing.

She was giving the song more than it asked for.

Wish Upon A Moon.
I made a wish to the moon.
Today was hard again.
I'll let myself be weak in front of you.
That's okay, right?

The lyrics leaned a little melancholy, but the feeling was kept so light that anyone could just enjoy it. Any age, any mood.

The drums and bass walked alongside a repeating violin line. A synth whistle carried the rhythmic melody on top. Behind everything: water drop sounds, frequency effects, layered in to build a kind of engineered fullness.

We had talked about this when we wrote the concept. The goal was distance. Ironic distance. Close enough to feel the sadness, far enough back to smile at it.

Wish Upon A Moon.
I'm wishing again today.
Share some of that shimmer with me.
So our darkness can lift.

When we put the concept together, we wanted it to hold something of Blue Sherbet's situation. To carry that weight honestly.

But not in a way that made anyone feel sorry for us.

And not in a way that wallowed.

Listen to the lyrics without paying attention and this was just a clean, trendy, genuinely addictive pop song. Nothing more.

"It would be strange if this didn't succeed."

Even the engineer kept exclaiming under his breath.

LOAN directing with precision. Hee-yeon catching every note of it and giving it back as voice.

The two of them were setting the studio on fire.

And honestly, that combination alone was already remarkable.

Add Jong-woo Kim and Eun-chul Lee as MV directors and I had assembled something extraordinary.

My job was simple: put the food in front of people. The finest ingredients, prepared by the best possible hands.

Just deal with whoever tried to ruin the dish along the way.

"If I can't pull this off, I've got no business being alive."

I'll say it again: I knew every move HS Entertainment was going to make.

You only had to get burned once or twice to learn.

***

The recording was raw. No mixing, no mastering. Still rough around the edges.

Jong-woo and Eun-chul heard through all of it to what it actually was.

"...Insane."

"Oh, this..."

Honestly, anyone with ears would have.

Before the regression, the public had consumed LOAN and Hee-yeon as a pair until they were practically sick of it, and they still went wild every single time. The combination never got old.

Now imagine hearing it for the very first time.

Two geniuses, appearing out of nowhere, hitting together like that.

I almost envied the people who got to experience it fresh.

"Boss. Is this really... something you can leave with us?"

Eun-chul's voice was serious.

Jong-woo had nothing on his name yet. But Eun-chul was already considered a prospect, someone to watch.

Curiosity about a new kind of project. Loyalty to a close friend. Interest in Yoon-jung and Hee-yeon. Those had all combined into a yes. But somewhere under that yes, there had been a quiet sense that this was beneath him. A step down.

Not anymore.

Now he was staring at the speaker wondering if he was good enough for it.

"Feeling the pressure? Worried you might not do it justice?"

I smiled when I asked. Eun-chul nodded without hesitation.

"Yes. Honestly... I wasn't that worried before. But now I am. No matter how hard we work, we still don't have real experience with music videos. That part doesn't change."

"Then step aside."

"...!"

Jong-woo.

His eyes were burning harder than I had ever seen them.

He held Eun-chul's gaze as he said it.

"Because I'm doing this no matter what."

"...Who said I was stepping aside, you idiot?"

"Boss. Sorry to ask, but could we use a little more of the budget?"

Out of nowhere.

"Sorry?"

"Enough to buy my guys some chicken. That's all."

"...?"

What he meant became clear soon enough.

The sun had set early. Somewhere in a back alley in Ilsan, only the moon and stars were out.

Two and three-story shopfronts ran down both sides of the street, every one of them dark. No lights on anywhere. It gave the whole place an unexpectedly good atmosphere.

Almost no foot traffic. And yet nearly ten students were moving through it with real urgency.

"Hey! Don't turn those lights on yet!"

"Sorry, yes! My bad!"

"Senior, this movement feels off. I don't think it's going to sit right against the storyboard."

"Ugh. Come on! Not here, over there!"

Watching from the side, I could already see that chicken was going to be nowhere near enough.

Eun-chul and Jong-woo had split the work without even discussing it. Eun-chul was running the location with total command: blocking, positioning, every call made fast and clean. Jong-woo had the song on loop, headphones in, revising the storyboard beat by beat.

*I'm going to have to let them eat and drink whatever they want when this is done.*

The setup was getting close to finished.

Right on cue, the Starex rolled up and stopped.

Every head swung toward it.

They all knew who was about to get out.

The door slid open. A long pair of legs extended first. Yoon-jung stepped down onto the pavement.

"Hello, everyone. Wow, this place has a really good vibe."

Eun-chul and Jong-woo's eyes went wide.

I could read their minds perfectly.

*She's even prettier in person.*

That was what everyone said the first time they saw Yoon-jung. Every single time.

Next out, wearing a slight pout, was Yumi.

"Why is my gummy candy in your pocket, unnie? Hi, everyone!"

Jin-ah climbed out behind her, dropping an arm around Yumi's shoulder.

Already smiling.

"Come on, I got mixed up. Ice cream later, my treat. Come on."

"No thanks. How do you get mixed up?"

"Are you sulking? I said I'm sorry. Anyway, hi, everyone!"

Yumi, quietly indignant over stolen candy. Jin-ah, completely unrepentant, face full of mischief.

Some of Jong-woo's juniors were watching them with these huge, dopey grins.

Yeah. Sharp instincts, those two.

Two complete fans, moving in perfect sync. There was something genuinely satisfying about it. I had been watching that chemistry develop for a while now.

One of these days it was going to erupt.

I was looking forward to it.

And last: the star of the evening.

She stepped out of the car and I heard breath catch all around me at once.

The other members had already pulled wide eyes. But Hee-yeon had done herself up today.

Black sneakers. Black denim. White tee. Purple bomber jacket.

On paper, nothing flashy.

"Wow..."

"Oh my god... Are you serious."

"Whoa."

Everything else, though.

The way the clothes moved on her was in a different category entirely. Cat eyes. A face that could read as cold if you didn't know her. She had been to the salon earlier in the day and it showed. Her skin was luminous, one side of her hair tucked behind her ear, the rest falling in a smooth silk curtain that made the whole look.

Right now, Hee-yeon could throw on a tracksuit and make it look like a luxury brand.

"Hello. Thank you for having me today."

She bowed. Nobody on set moved. They were just standing there, completely gone.

I found myself curious what was going to happen when she changed into the yellow sundress later.

I was still quietly watching the reaction when Jin-ah materialized at my side.

"Boss! Murphy's Law strikes again. The old saying never lies!"

"What are you talking about."

"Up there!"

She pointed at the sky.

Dark clouds. Some of them.

Surely not.

The forecast hadn't said anything about rain.

"Stop jinxing it. It's not going to rain. And even if it does, we'll be wrapped before it starts."

"Hmm. I don't know. Feels like it's coming."

"Would you stop."

But the old saying never lies.

Jin-ah's words were seeds, and inside an hour those seeds had taken root, shot up, and borne fruit.

*Whoooosh.*

The sky opened. Rain in sheets. We stood under a shop awning and stared at it.

"...Worst weather luck imaginable."

I breathed out slowly.

Our MV had a tight budget. No dance sequence. The concept didn't require many locations.

The plan was two: this alley tonight, and a studio booked for tomorrow. Locations weren't the problem. The problem was equipment rentals, props, hair and makeup and styling. All of it on the clock.

One day pushed meant money out of my pocket. Real money.

*Can't be helped.*

I'd eat the loss.

I was about to tell the directors to pack it in.

"Directors, let's go ahead and..."

Then Jin-ah spoke up.

"Boss! We've got a camera right here. Can I try something?"

"Hm?"

"You know that movie! The b-boy one. I've always wanted to dance in the rain. Like whoosh! Kicking up the water from the ground, going like this!"

"Go home, turn on the shower, dance to your heart's content. You'll get sick out here."

"...You have zero sense of romance."

Romance, my foot.

That was the thought in my head. Right before Eun-chul suddenly straightened up like Archimedes in the bath.

"Yes! That's it!"

"..."

I looked at Jin-ah without thinking.

She looked back at me with an expression of total vindication.

Something about that bothered me. It felt like I had lost.

I turned to Director Eun-chul, thoroughly unconvinced.

"You're not actually planning to use that idea directly, are you? Our song doesn't have a dance in it."

"That's not what I mean, Boss."

Eun-chul explained. Jong-woo and the juniors were nodding before he even finished.

And then, eventually, I was nodding too.

Director Eun-chul.

Every bit of trust placed in him, repaid.

Maybe. No. Definitely. The rain was going to help us.

"I was right, wasn't I, Boss?"

Jin-ah stood there, chin up, shoulders back, radiating satisfaction.

"You weren't right about a single thing."

"Look at you, refusing to admit it."

"There's nothing to admit."

Dancing in the rain and standing under soft light with a clear vinyl umbrella were two entirely different things. There was no connection between them whatsoever.

Either way, the storyboard they had spent all that time building got scrapped entirely. The camera settled in under the awning.

Hee-yeon stood out in the rain holding a transparent vinyl umbrella. The lights came up low.

What appeared in the frame stopped me.

Solitude? Elegance? Both at once.

In this old, empty market alley, she was radiant. Alone.

Every shop light off. The street bleak and cold. The rain coming down hard. And her, glowing in the middle of it.

Jong-woo still wasn't satisfied.

"Hmm... Something's not quite there."

"What are you talking about."

"Hey. Is there something else we can get?"

"Our props are useless, the whole plan is gone, and what we have now is better than anything we planned for. That's luck. Learn to take it."

"Hmm."

Jong-woo's low, dissatisfied sound made me look at the frame again.

And I got it immediately.

*Acting.*

There was one more talent still inside her.

I stopped the shoot.

"Hee-yeon. Come here."

"Yes."

She folded the umbrella and came under the awning. I took hold of her shoulders.

"Act for me."

"Act... for you?"

"Yeah. Take what's actually happening right now and push it. Exaggerate it. Today, we scraped together every last coin we had to be here. If the rain doesn't stop, we can't shoot, we have to close the company. Everyone splits up. And I go back to a rooftop room and drink myself to nothing."

Everyone nearby was looking at me like I had lost my mind.

"We have no hope left. All we can do is pray. So you're praying to the moon. Please stop the rain. Please let us shoot this MV."

"...Boss, that's really bad."

"Quiet! Am I a writer? And for her, I think this is..."

For Hee-yeon, this should be plenty.

While Jin-ah's blunt verdict was getting nods from everyone around her, Hee-yeon was somewhere else entirely.

She tilted her head a few times. Something moved across her face.

A small smile. Then sadness slipping into her eyes. Then something tight and furious underneath.

All of it, in a single instant.

"...!"

"...!"

Maybe that direction was exactly right for where she was.

She wasn't trained yet. Not enough to slide into a new character through craft alone. She had to feel her way there.

That was why it had to be that scenario.

"Unnie...?"

"What?"

Every person on set turned to look at her.

Something was rising off of her. Something without a name for it.

The ability to pull every eye in the room without asking. To bend the atmosphere of a space using only your face. The talent that belongs specifically to actors. To very few of them.

She was working with nothing but instinct, trying to absorb my rough, clumsy brief and make it into something real.

When she had enough of a sense of it, she opened the umbrella without a word and walked back to her spot.

The crew went quiet. Rolling again.

"Our unnie is insane..."

"Wow..."

We got the greatest shot of our lives.

"...Was that actually what I was asking for?"

Maybe I had a little talent as a writer. Or a director.
