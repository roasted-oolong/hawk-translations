# The Second Wave

"Jung-ho! Hey! You need to head to the concert!"

"Hm? Oh, right."

Only after the shout did Jung-ho Yoon finally pull out his earphones.

His manager let out a pointed sigh.

"I keep telling you not to listen so loud. Your ears and vocal cords are your livelihood."

"Ah, sorry."

Jung-ho smiled sheepishly, wound the earphone cord around his phone, and stuffed it into his pocket.

His manager cleared his throat quietly.

"I'm saying this because I worry, you know."

"I know. I've got this bad habit of cranking the volume whenever I find a good song. Can't seem to break it."

"What were you listening to?"

"The one sitting at number seven on the chart right now. 'Mistake.'"

"Oh!"

The manager nodded enthusiastically, then seemed to remember something and tilted his head.

"I know it's a good song, but… you hate EDM. And you don't like idol music either."

Jung-ho Yoon sat at the top of the domestic R&B scene.

His taste in music was ironclad. Blues, jazz, rock. All genres treated as niche in Korea, which meant his playlists were inevitably dominated by international artists. Pop he could tolerate, maybe, but idol music and EDM were well outside his orbit.

To the manager's question, Jung-ho curled one corner of his mouth upward.

"I don't know either. Just stumbled across it and liked what I heard."

"…Well, fair enough. It is a good song."

Jung-ho, his manager, and over a dozen staff members departed for the concert venue in a convoy of cars.

Just as they pulled out of the parking garage, the manager spoke up as if something had suddenly occurred to him.

"Oh! Jung-ho. Did you hear about this? Yumi Cho, the one who made 'Mistake.' She's putting together a full-length album right now and looking for artists to feature. The whole thing's turned into a circus."

"A circus?"

"Yeah. Singers and rappers are falling over themselves posting videos on SNS. It's insane."

Jung-ho's interest sharpened. Not for any complicated reason. He was simply curious what Yumi's new music sounded like.

He searched her name on a portal site, and a flood of information poured out. Normally, when one piece of news was trending, that topic dominated the results. Yumi was different. Conversations about her variety show, the OST, her acoustic track, and this album were all erupting at the same time.

"She's blowing up, huh."

"Blowing up is an understatement! The whole music scene is losing its mind. SBC's completely lit up over it. Other variety shows are fighting tooth and nail to book Blue Sherbet too."

"I've been completely out of the loop on what's been going on lately."

"You can afford to be. Your position's untouchable. You don't need to worry about any of that. Just make whatever music you want and the fans keep loving you."

"I'm grateful for it."

Jung-ho found what he was looking for easily enough. It was the hottest topic around.

He reached for his earphones out of habit, then stopped and played the video through the phone's speaker instead.

A six-minute clip.

With every passing minute, he turned the volume up a little more.

When it ended, he spoke.

"Let me have the earphones for just six minutes."

"…Keep it low, please."

"Got it."

He plugged in and hit replay.

How many times did he loop it? Eventually he pulled the earphones out and stared blankly out the window.

"We're here."

The concert venue.

The show began on schedule. About two hours in, tens of thousands of fans had sunk deep into Jung-ho Yoon's performance.

Then, out of nowhere, he said something no one expected.

"Everyone, I'm sorry. I shouldn't be doing this, but something keeps popping into my head between songs. I feel like I haven't been fully present with you tonight."

The audience was bewildered. He'd delivered a flawless live performance worthy of Korea's top R&B vocalist. What did he mean, not fully present?

Voices erupted from the crowd.

"That's not true!"

"It was amazing!"

"We'll be the judge of that!"

"You're the best, Jung-ho!"

"Love you!"

Jung-ho spoke again.

"There isn't much time left in the show, but I want to be completely focused for whatever's remaining. So I need to get this thing out of my head first. I apologize again."

Huh?

Confusion spread across the audience's faces.

"Oh, and if you could film this and upload it online, I'd appreciate it."

He closed his eyes and began to sing.

"…Wait."

"What!?"

Most of the crowd's eyes went wide with shock. They understood exactly what it meant for him to be singing this particular song.

"That's Yumi's song!"

"Jung-ho's throwing his hat in too? That's insane! This is huge!"

"…Game over, then."

Three songs across three minutes.

A heavyweight had entered the war of artists competing for a spot on Yumi's album.

\*\*

Golden Tree, the boy group where Min-seok served as main rapper.

Hyun-woo was their lead vocalist. He'd been passed over for main vocalist because another member had higher range. But fans and members alike acknowledged that Hyun-woo had the best vocal tone in the group, bar none.

"You're watching more of those videos again? It's out of your hands now. Staring at your phone won't change anything. Just wait quietly until the results come in."

Min-seok had just finished practice with Kid. He called out to Hyun-woo, who sat scrolling through videos other artists had posted.

Hyun-woo didn't even look up.

"Jung-ho Yoon just posted his too."

"…That's bad."

"Yeah. When Yumi picks her vocalists, she won't care about fandom size. So this is really bad."

Min-seok went quiet. Hyun-woo let out a sigh and continued.

"Even just one minute and it stays stuck in your head. These songs… they're going to be incredible."

"You can do it. Your tone doesn't lose to anyone's."

"I just hope Yumi sees something she likes in mine."

Hyun-woo finally set down the phone he'd been glued to.

That was when he noticed Min-seok's expression. Completely different from his own. Bright.

"Practice going well? Good chemistry with Kid?"

A deep smile settled on Min-seok's lips.

"Smooth sailing. The song's amazing too. Different genre, but purely in terms of quality, it holds its own against any of Yumi's tracks."

"True. It really is good. It's going to be a hit."

\*\*

"Our boss really knows how to work the angles. Builds the hype, gets the result, never lifts a finger."

Inside the K Management office.

Manager Jang snorted at Team Lead Na's remark.

"This is way beyond hype. The fandoms are at war with each other. It's not just one or two, either. They're literally drawing blood."

Team Lead Na burst out laughing.

Manager Jang stared at him, startled by the intensity of the reaction.

"You're that happy about it?"

"Of course I am! Artists are falling all over themselves just to be on our Yumi's album! Nobody looks at Yumi like some rookie idol anymore! You'd think someone would say 'isn't this beneath me?' But do the fans say that? Do the artists? Nobody does! They just want their singer to be the one picked, so they go to war with other fandoms over it."

Now that Manager Jang understood what was behind the laughter, a chuckle of his own slipped out.

"And that's the smart play. Some of the artists who didn't participate are probably the ones whose pride wouldn't let them. They'll regret it like crazy once they hear the full versions, but by then it'll be too late."

"Exactly! The one-minute previews are just a taste of how incredible the full tracks are. That kid is a genuine genius. Her growth rate is unreal."

"If I hear that one more time, I'm going to start bleeding from my ears."

\*\*

Filming day.

I drove the van toward the dorm.

Climbed the villa stairs slowly.

The car had changed. The practice room had changed. The office had changed. We even had a proper studio now. But this place stayed the same.

The company had expanded. My position and the members' positions kept climbing. But this place stayed the same.

Just as the members had grown attached to it, so had I.

Now a hazy memory. Taking Blue Sherbet out of HS, debuting them from this place, and watching it all collapse.

And the day I regressed. Coming back here with Jin-ah.

Whether we were at our lowest or riding high, every trace of us lived here.

I punched in the door code and opened it. Quiet air greeted me.

"Hey, you're here?"

A face brimming with mischief.

Jin-ah kept her voice low as she said hello.

"Knock it off with the attitude."

I passed the entryway into the living room. Nobody in sight.

Hee-yeon and Yoon-jung being out on schedules made sense, but Yumi always came padding over the moment I arrived, greeting me first.

"Is Yumi sleeping?"

We needed to leave for the shoot. Don't tell me she hadn't even washed up.

As I stood there puzzled, Jin-ah smiled and answered.

"She finished getting ready and just closed her eyes for a minute."

The fact that she still hadn't come out of her room meant she must have drifted off.

Yesterday her only schedule had been vocal training. Nothing else.

"Did she not sleep well?"

I tilted my head, and that was when I caught it. The corner of Jin-ah's mouth was twitching.

What's going on?

Suspicious.

I narrowed my eyes and fixed a hard stare on her. The trembling spread from her lips to her eyes, which were shaking like a small earthquake had hit.

Did she not realize that stubbornly holding eye contact was making her look more suspicious, not less?

"You're hiding something."

"N-nooo!?"

She was.

"Tell me straight. Did you two… fight?"

The twitching stopped.

Replaced by a deep frown.

"Fight? Who's fighting! I'm so nice to her! The poor thing is already—"

Jin-ah's breath caught.

"'Poor thing'?"

I glanced toward the room Yumi shared with Yoon-jung, then jerked my chin at Jin-ah.

Toward the room she shared with Hee-yeon.

Jin-ah followed me with heavy steps.

"Sit down."

"…Is this an interrogation?"

"Then stand."

She sat in the room's single chair. Defeat was written all over her face.

"'Poor thing.' What does that mean?"

Yumi was riding the biggest wave of her career. What was there to feel sorry about?

*No. Wait.*

Everyone was calling Yumi a genius, heaping praise on her. Established artists were fighting fiercely to be on her album. The response to "Mistake" was still white-hot. People kept asking when she'd release the song she'd made with Hae-yeol Yoo.

I asked, dreading the answer.

"Don't tell me Yumi's been feeling pressured?"

Jin-ah shook her head violently.

Her eyes met mine without a flicker of hesitation, insisting that wasn't it.

"No! That's really not it! After vocal training she comes home and practices guitar, and she keeps rewatching the videos other artists posted! She says she needs to find inspiration, so she's trying all sorts of things, and she just doesn't have time to sleep! That's all!"

…That was pressure.

Pushing herself because she couldn't bear to disappoint the expectations around her.

I dragged a hand down my face.

"How long has this been going on?"

Since the last filming, she'd only been shuttling between vocal training, the dorm, and the studio. We hadn't seen each other face to face.

"…"

Jin-ah looked up at me with a pained expression.

She grabbed my folded arm and clung to it.

"Yumi made me promise not to tell you! She said it would only take a little longer! Pulling a few all-nighters for work is totally normal. Everyone does it. That's all this is!"

So it wasn't just one night. She'd barely slept for days.

"…Fine."

"Don't you dare tell her I said anything! Promise me!"

Her grip on my arm tightened.

Her other hand shot toward my face, pinky extended.

From the look in her eyes, she had no intention of letting me leave this room without a pinky swear.

"Okay. Promise. Promise."

"Stamp it."

Did we really have to do something this childish?

"Stamp it!"

"Why not ask for a copy while you're at it."

"That's childish. Who does that?"

I had nothing to say to that.

I pressed my thumb against hers obediently. Jin-ah's face relaxed into relief.

She even let out a long breath and patted her own chest.

Maybe I should just tell Yumi outright.

"I was sleeping in here the whole time. Got it?"

I left her room and headed for Yumi's.

Knock knock.

"…"

I opened the door.

She was lying with her legs hanging off the edge of the bed, positioned to spring up at a moment's notice.

Soft, even breathing. Eyes closed.

"Yumi."

"…"

"Yumi."

Her eyelids rose slowly.

She blinked at me, still foggy.

"Boss…?"

"Time to go. We've got filming."

She blinked a few more times, then sat bolt upright.

Rubbed her eyes briefly. Then smiled.

A sleepy, gentle grin.

"Let's go."

I nodded and headed down the villa stairs.

Got in the van and quietly took the wheel.

Yumi settled into the passenger seat, same as always.

I'd gone to the trouble of buying a van and a Carnival, and the members still fought over the passenger seat.

Before rounding the corner, I stole a glance. She was on her phone, scrolling through the song videos artists had been posting.

I kept my voice casual.

"You haven't eaten yet, have you?"

"Oh, no. Have you?"

"Me neither."

A brief calculation in my head.

Very brief.

The decision came in an instant.

Maybe it was impulsive. I didn't have a clear plan.

"Yumi. Want to grab some jeyuk-deopbap?"

*(T/N: Jeyuk-deopbap is a popular Korean dish of spicy stir-fried pork served over rice.)*

"What?"

Her gaze went from her phone to me.

She asked in a flustered voice.

"Won't we be late for filming?"

I worked hard to make my smile look mischievous.

"We do all this to eat, right? What's it matter. A little detour once in a while never hurt."

"I, I'm really fine! I'm not even that hungry…!"

What had started as an impulsive remark began assembling itself into a plan as I spoke. The puzzle pieces were clicking together on their own.

I needed to take some of the weight off her shoulders.

"I'm the hungry one. And you like jeyuk-deopbap too."

"But… everyone will be waiting for us."

I'd already turned the wheel.

Not toward the filming location. Toward the jeyuk-deopbap place.

"I'll call and say sorry. Honestly, it's the production team's fault for scheduling a shoot at a time that's awkward for eating."

I shifted the blame. For a completely nonsensical reason.

"And who goes through life doing everything by the book? You disappoint your parents, break promises, talk behind people's backs, get into fights. You fail sometimes too. That's just how it goes."

The van rolled on. Yumi was quiet.

All I heard was her tapping at her phone. Tap, tap, tap.

I kept talking.

"You know, when I first started this company, everyone seemed to think I'd make it big right awa—"

A short vibration. A message from Jin-ah.

[I told you not to tell her it was me!!! You stamped the promise and you're breaking it already ARE YOU SERIOUS!!!]

Huh? I never said anything.

Another message, right on its heels.

[Yumi's asking me why I told youㅠㅠㅠㅠㅠ]

"…"

My phone was mounted on the dashboard.

Which meant Yumi, sitting in the passenger seat, could see every word.

I turned my head slightly. Sure enough, she was looking at my phone too.

But her reaction wasn't what I expected. She was grinning.

"Boss, you're really bad at this."

Damn.

Awkward. Embarrassing.

I'd been trying to gently show her she didn't need to carry that kind of pressure.

"An elementary schooler would've seen through that."

She was laughing, genuinely amused.

A relief, if nothing else. But still.

"This is what happens when you make people worry. Walking around with pressure you don't need."

"I just wanted to do better. That's all it was."

"When the people around you start worrying, that's how you know you've crossed the line."

"…Boss, so are we getting jeyuk or not?"

"…What do you think?"

I felt her gaze from the passenger seat. She'd turned her head fully to stare at my face.

"Can we go to the studio instead? I think I'm getting an idea right now. I might actually be able to finish the album."

"What!?"

Where did the inspiration even come from?

"But I guess we can't, right? Today's filming schedule doesn't include studio time."

Can't? What do you mean, can't?

"I'll call and say sorry. Actually… they'll probably be thrilled."

Yumi's first full-length album.

I had no idea where this second wave of inspiration had come from, but the album was about to be completed.
