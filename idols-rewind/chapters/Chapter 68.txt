Table Read (1)

Jung-han Yoo eased the van into a parking space.

He cut the engine, and the car went quiet.

Hee-yeon sat there with her battered script in her hands, looking at me blankly. As if wondering why I wasn't getting out.

"Hee-yeon. Acting works a little differently from music. There's the status games between actors. In music you pass each other once and that's the whole relationship, but here you're on one project together, seeing each other over and over. Sharing the spotlight, too. So somebody might try to set the pecking order right here at the table read."

"Who might?"

"The actors. Don't get too worried about it, though."

Hee-yeon nodded.

"Okay. I won't worry."

She'd never been through it and never even heard about it, so she probably couldn't picture what those rooms were actually like. Not a grain of worry in her voice.

I let out a small sigh and kept going.

"I'd help you if I could, but I might not be able to step in directly. If I get involved, I'm just handing everyone else a reason to get involved too. It could blow up. So no matter who keeps picking at you, don't shrink and don't take it to heart. Whatever happens before the read, you shut them down with your reading."

"Understood. I won't shrink and I won't take it to heart."

An immediate answer, perfectly level. She seemed to think it would be nothing.

*...Fine. Better than being scared before anything happens.*

She wasn't walking in blind, and I'd said everything there was to say. I wanted to worry, but this was the kind of thing she had to be able to take. If she ever ended up on the wrong end of the public and the press someday, building her up in stages like this was what would keep her from crumbling all at once. Not that it would ever happen, not while I was watching.

"Let's go."

Jung-han, Hee-yeon, and I got out and headed for the elevator. Up ahead, a Carnival was just finishing its park. The door slid open and a good-looking man stepped out.

Sung-hyun Park. Co-lead of our film alongside Sung-ah, and a veteran top-tier actor whose reputation was built entirely on his acting. He'd be forty any day now, but he had the face of a man in his late twenties, and even through his clothes you could see how solid his build was.

We stopped where we were and waited for him to reach us.

"Hello, sir. I'm Hee-yeon Lee."

His eyes curved slightly. The voice that came out was clear enough to pull your attention in on its own.

"Good to meet you."

He greeted me too without breaking stride toward the elevator.

"Good to meet you."

Sung-hyun Park passed us by. We fell in behind him.

Fourth floor... third... second... first... basement one.

We got in without a word and pressed the fourth floor.

"Saw the video. You were good."

The thing that broke the silence was a compliment.

"Thank you."

"I heard you only started learning recently. That true?"

"Yes. My promotions ended and Sung-ah Kim taught me for the first time."

"Wow. You'll grow a lot shooting this. Show us something good from the start if you can."

"Yes. I will."

The elevator doors opened. We followed Sung-hyun Park into the read-through room.

A big, booming voice burst out of him.

"Sir! Haha. Why are you here so early again? I figured you'd come early this time too, so I made sure to get here early myself. Didn't want you sitting around bored. Hahaha!"

Shameless charm, and plenty of it. There'd been no sign of that side of him a minute ago.

"I like the air at the start of a film. That's why I came early."

"It's just stuffy in here, what's good about that? You're free after the read, right? I've got a place all set up so we can have a drink. Their braised mackerel is unbelievable."

"Braised mackerel? Where? That place out front?"

"Come on! No comparison."

Hee-yeon walked over to them and bowed deeply.

"Hello, sir. I'm Hee-yeon Lee."

I greeted him after her.

Chul-hwan Son. A little past sixty, with more than forty years of acting behind him.

"Ah, yes. Good to meet you. I saw the video. You were good."

"Thank you."

The audition video again. Releasing it had been the right call.

"Don't worry so much about only starting recently," Chul-hwan Son said. "Work hard and you'll come a long way."

"Thank you."

"Idol, beginner, none of it matters. Show good acting and that settles it. You know what I mean? Keep the fire in you all the way to the end. Don't burn out."

"Yes. Thank you."

Chul-hwan Son gave her a small smile and went back to talking with Sung-hyun Park. Hee-yeon greeted everyone else who'd already arrived, one by one, then sat down at her assigned seat and opened her script.

We stood well back from them. Jung-han lowered his voice.

"There aren't any status games, hyungnim."

"Why would those two need to check Hee-yeon? It's the actor who hasn't shown up yet that's the problem."

"Ah. Hyungnim, there's something I've been wondering about. May I ask?"

"We just walked into the room and you've already got another question?"

"You're one of the investors in this film, and you brought in Yoon-jung and Yumi, and Sung-ah Kim on top of that. You've contributed a lot. So I doubt the other actors would even try to touch Hee-yeon."

I let out a small snort.

"Investment? As a share of the whole thing, mine is a few percent. We're already at the table read. I don't have the power to swap out an actor. And the rest of it might mean something to the production side, but to the actors it's none of their business. Treat the status games as their own separate thing. If people in this business stopped to tally up what each agency contributed, the games would have died out a long time ago."

***

"Hah... this is so annoying, seriously."

The screen in her hand scrolled and scrolled. In the search bar was the director's name, 'Min-woo Sung.'

Tap tap. She changed the search. The film title, 'Could I Borrow a Little of Your Luck?' The scrolling started again. Irritation was caked all over her face.

"Oppa, why does my name barely come up? Why isn't the company pushing me at all!"

"I'll talk to the PR team about it, Mi-yeon."

Mi-yeon Chu turned wide, glittering eyes on her manager.

"There's a limit to not pushing someone. I search the film and all that comes up is Sung-hyun Park, Sung-ah Kim, Hee-yeon Lee. Yoon-jung Hwang and Yumi Cho get mentioned more than I do. Does that make any sense? Does it! Why isn't my name in there!"

"No, no. Of course your name's in there. It isn't that bad. Really."

The fire in her eyes flared. The manager felt like he was dying. There was nowhere to escape inside a car, and the team leader had clearly seen this coming, since he'd invented some hurried excuse and skipped the trip. Now he had to calm her down alone, and he had nothing to work with. She really was barely mentioned. Less than Yoon-jung Hwang and Yumi Cho, who weren't even in the film.

"Damn it! Like an audition tape is some big deal. She doesn't have the basics, she just rides one flashy line and pretends she can act. Makes me sick. People's eyes must be broken. They don't know acting when they see it."

"...It's because she's an idol, so the bar was on the floor. And the line was so loaded that they mistook nothing for something good. Once she's actually on screen she'll get buried in insults. Her CEO is an investor, so they probably burned through dozens of takes just to scrape that video together."

The flame in Mi-yeon Chu's eyes died down a little.

"Right? And Sung-ah Kim drilled that scene into her line by line."

"A hundred percent!"

Her face relaxed. She pulled a deep breath in and let it out long and slow.

"Cocky little brat."

Before telling her it was time to head up, the manager brought up the thing he had to warn her about. Carefully, watching her face.

"Ahem. Mi-yeon, uh... I'm only saying this because I don't want you distracted during the read today. Let's not pay attention to anything else."

"What? What's that supposed to mean?"

"...Putting Hee-yeon Lee in her place, save it for later. Somewhere the two of you are alone would be better, I think. That brat's a sly one. She might turn it into politics."

Mi-yeon Chu's face twisted. The manager swallowed and pushed on.

"You know you don't have to play nice with Sung-ah Kim just because she's a senior, right? Don't go and-"

"I know that! Who doesn't know what she's like!"

"Ha, haha. Right? Oh! And just in case, since Sung-ah Kim and Hee-yeon Lee might be close-"

"Don't touch Hee-yeon Lee in front of Sung-ah Kim? Because she might defend her?"

A dry laugh burst out of Mi-yeon Chu.

"Are you joking, oppa? That psycho, defending somebody? Do you think that makes any sense?"

Ah. Now that he thought about it, he'd never heard anything like that either. The manager decided he shouldn't have brought it up.

"Right... no chance that lunatic would. Still, let's keep it quiet today. Okay? Please. I'm asking you."

***

Sung-hyun Park and Chul-hwan Son were both in a position to stop the status games before they started, or to step in and mediate. Neither of them had any intention of doing so. Some actors can't stand watching the mood get muddied, but temperaments differ from actor to actor. From decades of knocking around film sets, they knew that mediating a status fight didn't actually settle it. It only held for that one moment. They wouldn't be seeing each other for a day or two, and they wouldn't always be gathered in the same room.

*Better to have the order settled early. Cleaner for everyone down the line.*

It might feel suffocating if you were the one at the bottom, but taken as a whole, a set only ran smoothly once the order was fixed.

Sung-hyun Park watched Mi-yeon Chu come into the read-through room.

"Everyone! Hello! I'm looking forward to working with you all."

A syrupy voice and a manufactured smile. Depending on how you looked at it, that was still manners. A hundred times better than letting her real nature out and being rude to everybody.

Every eye in the room went to her. Because she was pretty? No, not only that. If pretty was all it took, nobody would have been able to look away from Hee-yeon Lee's face whether Mi-yeon Chu walked in or not. They were watching her because every single one of them had heard about her temper at least once.

And sure enough, exactly as everyone expected, she headed straight for the rookie getting more attention than she was. When Hee-yeon Lee had greeted her on the way in, she hadn't so much as glanced her way. Now she spoke as if no greeting had ever happened.

"Oh my? Hello there. My name is Mi-yeon Chu, in case you didn't know."

An exaggerated delivery. You could almost hear the line that came after it. *And why didn't you say hello to me?*

The air went tight. In a room where only breathing could be heard, everyone was watching the two of them.

"Hello, ma'am. I'm Hee-yeon Lee."

She'd forced the greeting out of her a second time.

"You must have been buried in your script."

"No."

"No? Then you saw me come in?"

"Yes."

"...Are your answers always that short? And do you always look people straight in the eye like that?"

"Yes. Mostly."

"Ha! Look how bold you are. Are you talking back to a senior?"

"No."

Eyes with a slight upward slant that read cool and aloof, a gaze that didn't waver, a posture that didn't shrink. Hee-yeon looked steadily at Mi-yeon Chu and went on answering.

An exchange like that should have wound the tension tight enough to punch through the ceiling. Because of Hee-yeon Lee, it wasn't happening. A snort of laughter slipped out of Sung-hyun Park. Mi-yeon Chu's head whipped around at the sound, then turned back once she saw who it was.

Why was that? It didn't look like a status fight. It looked like shadow boxing. Like watching someone jab a finger at a wall.

Everyone in the room swallowed their laughter and watched Hee-yeon Lee with real interest. It was strange. She answered in those short, steady little words and somehow it never sounded snide, never sounded like talking back, didn't even come across as rude. She was simply calm. No damage was landing. The thought surfaced that maybe she was Buddhist.

Every person in that room was thinking it about her. Every person but one.

"...Why are you so rude?"

Mi-yeon Chu's face had gone scarlet. With everyone watching, she couldn't stop now. Stopping here would look like tucking her tail.

"...."

"Now you won't even answer?"

"Because I'm not sure."

"About what."

"I'm not sure whether I was rude. I was thinking about it."

"Hey!"

This had stopped being a status game and was turning into a plain argument. Though all it meant was that the punches at empty air would come harder.

Just as Mi-yeon Chu's face looked ready to burst, the door of the read-through room opened and someone new walked in.

Team Lead Kwak from HS Entertainment's Management Team 3. And Sung-ah Kim.

Her eyes went to Hyuk Kang.

"Hm? Oppa, what's with that face? Something good happen?"
