Chapter 10 - The Greatest Singer, Hae-chun Lee

Single release.

Established artists would have carefully chosen a release date, run teasers, built up promotion well in advance. We had no need for any of that.

All we could do was go live for the handful of fans still following us, and put up a post on the official fan cafe.

We weren't doing any music show appearances, so the fans didn't need to prepare anything in advance either.

So the moment our slot on *Yeol's Music Notebook* was confirmed, we went straight to publishing.

No banner on the streaming sites. No featured placement on the main page. We already knew that was coming.

Which was exactly why we had to show up on *Yeol's Music Notebook* and make it count.

***

"Please keep it simple. Clean and fresh — not too flashy."

"I know, I know. You really do worry a lot, CEO Kang."

We had picked the outfit well in advance, and stopped at a salon to get her hair and makeup done.

When it was all finished, she was, as always, blindingly beautiful.

"Hae-yeol Yoo will take care of you — he knows how to put guests at ease. Just think of it as singing a song and having a chat with the guy next door."

"I know. I've been on the show before."

"That was as a group, and Jin-ah did most of the talking. It might feel different this time."

"I'll be fine. I'm not nervous."

"..."

Then maybe try opening the water bottle before drinking from it.

She'd been pressing the sealed cap to her lips for the past minute. Not nervous, she said.

Hee-yeon's face was perfectly calm. Every other part of her was a wreck.

We arrived at the studio and headed for the waiting room.

***

Hee-yeon was first on the lineup.

The running order had nothing to do with quality — it was sorted by debut history and name recognition.

But if she could hold herself together, none of that would matter. She'd be the one everyone remembered today.

*"Any couples in the audience today? Raise your hands."*

Hae-yeol Yoo's voice filled the studio.

The audience buzzed with excitement.

The air was warm and close.

Time moved fast. Before long, we were waiting in the wings.

Hee-yeon stood with her mouth closed tight, adjusting her in-ear monitor with a blank expression. She looked like she was somewhere else.

From the outside, she showed nothing. But I knew her mind had gone completely white.

"Hee-yeon. You remember the MV shoot?"

"Yes."

"You remember how you found that emotion?"

"Yes."

"Bring that back. Right now, you're not Hee-yeon Lee — you're a character named Hae-chun Lee. The singer Hae-chun Lee. Hae-chun Lee wants to give this audience the greatest performance of their lives. She's so deep in the feeling of this song that she pulls everyone in with her. You understand?"

"...Not Hee-yeon Lee... Hae-chun Lee... Hae-chun Lee."

"Hae-chun Lee is the greatest singer. She's always confident. She never gets nervous. You're playing Hae-chun Lee."

"...Okay. I'll try."

She murmured *Hae-chun Lee, Hae-chun Lee* quietly to herself, then got the signal from a staff member and walked out onto the stage.

Her stride to the center was completely steady.

***

*Waaaah.*

The audience's practiced applause and cheers.

Blue light fell over her as the backing track began to play through the studio.

Hee-yeon stood perfectly still at the center of the stage, eyes closed, head slightly bowed.

I could see the crew starting to panic. The audience was tilting their heads, a few smothering smiles.

They thought she'd frozen from nerves.

The track kept playing.

Then the moment arrived for her to sing.

She lifted her head and raised the microphone to her lips.

*Wish Upon A Moon.*
*I made a wish to the moon.*
*Not because miracles actually happen — just in case.*
*I know they don't, but just in case.*

"Whoa!"

"Wait — what!"

"That voice!"

The crew let out a collective breath. The audience reacted instantly.

***

Hae-yeol had been floored when his assistant director played him the track.

Going into the taping, he was excited.

A real diamond in the rough — it had been a while since he'd seen one.

She'd performed on the show once before, with the group. The song had been good. But it hadn't really stayed with him.

This song, though. He had a feeling he wouldn't be forgetting it for a long time. Maybe ever.

He'd been listening to it on repeat.

*I wonder what the live will be like.*

He stood at the side of the stage, watching her at the center.

But then — was she too nervous?

The backing track had started, and she was completely frozen.

"Ah..."

A quiet, pained sigh escaped him.

But the disappointment lasted only a moment.

When she raised her head, the expression on her face was something he hadn't seen in years — not since the senior artists he'd looked up to as a young man.

And then she sang.

*Wish Upon A Moon.*

Catharsis ran up his spine and spread through his whole body.

*There it is.*

He'd started to wonder if he was getting old — modern songs, modern singers, none of them had given him this feeling in so long.

But the problem hadn't been with him.

How many years had he been running this program? How many live performances had he stood at the side of this stage and watched?

He had never seen a diamond in the rough like this.

Her expression and her eyes drew the audience in before a single note had landed. And her voice sank into everyone in that studio and didn't let go.

*Wish Upon A Moon.*
*I made a wish to the moon.*
*Today was hard again.*
*I'll let myself be a little weak in front of you.*
*That's okay, right?*

The audience responded without thinking. The bright, warm music put smiles on their faces, and the feeling in the lyrics settled gently into all of them.

*Wish Upon A Moon.*
*I'm making a wish again tonight.*
*Share a little of that glow with me.*
*So that our darkness can lift.*

Hae-yeol could not take his eyes off her.

He wanted to close them, to lose himself in just the sound — but he wanted to keep watching, too. He couldn't choose. So he kept his eyes open, and stayed.

It was over too soon.

He could have listened for hours. It felt criminal that it was this short.

Only after the song ended could he finally close his eyes and sit inside the feeling for a moment.

A few seconds. That was all he could steal.

He had a show to run. He couldn't stand there forever.

With an expression caught somewhere between deep longing and awe, he walked out to the center of the stage.

***

Staff brought chairs out. Hae-yeol sat, and Hee-yeon sat across from him.

"Hello — would you like to introduce yourself?"

She stood again and bowed deeply.

"Hello. I'm Hae-chun Lee, the greatest singer."

"...!"

"...?"

"...!?"

"Oh. I'm Hee-yeon Lee of Blue Sherbet."

She sat back down with a blank face and looked straight ahead, as if nothing had happened.

Audience, crew, and Hae-yeol alike needed a moment to blink.

"So... um... what just..."

Hae-yeol's baffled reaction made the audience start laughing. And then Hee-yeon refused to react at all, and they lost it entirely.

"Miss Hee-yeon."

"Yes, Senior."

"The greatest singer — who was that again?"

"...That was a mistake."

"But you just said —"

"Hae-chun Lee."

Something about the name felt oddly warm and familiar. The audience leaned in, suddenly very interested.

"Who is this person? The greatest singer? Is she — your mother, or..."

"No. My boss told me about her."

The audience cooled slightly.

*Was the whole thing scripted? Did the CEO set this up?*

Then Hee-yeon kept going.

"I was nervous, so he told me to pretend to be Hae-chun Lee, the greatest singer."

They were hooked again.

So was Hae-yeol.

"What does that even mean? Walk me through it."

Hee-yeon recited Hyuk's exact words, step by step.

The audience fell apart. Hae-yeol dropped his head and dissolved into his signature laugh — a helpless, choking sound that went on for a while.

"So — *hkk!* — what you're saying is — *kkk* — so then she —"

He couldn't get a sentence out. Just the laugh, gulping and gasping.

Eventually he composed himself enough to turn to the audience.

"She's a very unusual character, isn't she?"

"Yes!"

"Yes!"

***

"I had so much I wanted to say about the song — about the music. And then Hae-chun Lee shows up out of nowhere! That's not fair! What am I supposed to do with the feelings I had? With the moment I was in?"

We got lucky.

There was a real chance the audience could have found her non-reaction boring. But they hadn't — they'd taken to this character with open arms, even warmly.

"And to think it was all an act. A true music genius should have heard her and immediately known something was off. I missed it completely. But — and I want to be clear, everyone — that is not my fault. Hae-chun Lee is just that powerful. The greatest singer, after all."

"I'm sorry. That wasn't my intention."

The completely unprompted apology sent the audience into another round of laughter.

The room couldn't have been going better. Hee-yeon's head, on the other hand, was a full disaster. She genuinely couldn't figure out where things had gone wrong.

"Miss Hee-yeon! You are genuinely charming — everyone in this room is already a fan. I am too."

"Thank you. I'll work hard."

"Is this just how you normally are?"

"No."

"Sorry?"

"Sorry?"

"...Hm."

Hae-yeol laughed his way through it and moved on.

"Alright, let's put the comedy sketch on hold and talk about the music. How was this song made? It gave me a kind of nostalgia I haven't felt in a long time. Let me tell you what it was like for me."

He talked through everything — what he felt when he heard the track, what it brought up, what it meant. No filter.

The audience already knew what it meant when Hae-yeol talked about a song like this. They'd been watching him for years. This wasn't performance. This was the real thing.

And they'd all just heard the live themselves. They felt it too.

"I met with the composer, and we worked on it right then and there. My boss told me to sing a few of my favorite songs, and the composer built it from that, on the spot."

"That's incredible! Wait — that's how this happened?"

"Yes."

The audience wasn't sure what that meant. Hae-yeol was.

Because he was a composer himself.

"Everyone — this is actually extraordinary! Other composers and I can do this, sure, but tailoring something this precisely to the singer's voice and getting this level of quality? That is genuinely hard to pull off. Actually — let me just show you. Fair warning: I've only heard one song from her, so don't be too disappointed if this isn't very good."

He walked to the piano.

The audience knew exactly what was coming, and they screamed.

"Come here, Miss Hee-yeon. Follow my lead as I play — and I'll match the feel to you."

"...Okay."

"If it's too hard, go get Hae-chun Lee. She can handle it."

"...Right."

Producer Kim was grinning from ear to ear.

More than he could have hoped for. Every last bit of it.

Hae-yeol played, his singing voice leaving something to be desired, and Hee-yeon — Hae-chun Lee — caught his every instinct like she was reading his mind, and sang without holding anything back.

With the audience's response burning that hot, of course her recording time ran long.

There was no question about it: the star of the day was the first act. Hee-yeon Lee.

***

"This is so exciting! God! I can't take it! That's it — Miss Hee-yeon, once your promotions are done, let's work together."

"I'd be so honored. But I have to get permission from my boss."

"Hyuk! What do you say?"

Hae-yeol called out to Hyuk, who was standing at the side of the stage.

Hyuk gave him the OK.
