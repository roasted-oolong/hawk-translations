**Open arcs:**
- Regression setup complete: Kang has returned to the period just after Blue Sherbet's failed debut. His four members are intact and under his management. He does not yet know why he returned to this specific point rather than earlier.
- New strategy declared (Ch. 3): Kang suspends group activities and begins individual development tracks — Hee-yeon gets a solo song, Jin-ah focuses on vocal/dance training, Yun-jeong is pointed toward dance content and gaming for future YouTube/streaming, Yu-mi is directed to focus on composition and arrangement. The "Avengers" model (each member individually brilliant, synergy when combined) replaces his prior approach of treating the group as a unified unit first.
- Composer LOAN: Kang's stated first external action item is finding this currently-undiscovered composer. No details given yet; watch for introduction.

**Watch list:**
- The regression trigger is the full moon shimmering strangely (보름달의 빛이 기이하게 반짝거렸다) — no mechanics explained. If the full moon reappears as a motif, it may carry weight.
- Kang returned to *after* the failed debut, not the beginning of independence. The narrative does not explain why. This may become relevant.
- Kang slips in Ch. 3 — begins to say "느껴지는 게 있다고 했었" (you said you'd feel something) before catching himself and rephrasing to "느껴지는 게 있을 거야" (you'll feel something). He nearly reveals foreknowledge to Yu-mi. This is a live continuity risk — translator must preserve the self-correction and the awkward pivot.
- Hee-yeon's two versions (pre-regression top star vs. regression-era trainee) are set up as a deliberate contrast. Her speech patterns and emotional register differ between the two timelines — translator should track this carefully across chapters.
- Director Park's warning that Kang is too visible and will face industry retaliation is framed as genuine, not bluster. Likely sets up antagonist forces in the regression timeline.
- Kang explicitly criticizes his past self for being too cautious and deferential, and for over-relying on his talent instinct while underestimating structural/political obstacles. His new approach is explicitly more aggressive. This is the core behavioral change to track — scenes where he is tested on this should be translated to reflect the contrast with his past self clearly.

**Translation-relevant context:**
- Director Park's three-proverb sequence in Ch. 1 is a single rhetorical unit; the escalation from flattery to threat must read as a deliberate move, not three separate observations. See Cultural Phrases entries.
- Kang's internal assessment of each member in Ch. 3 mixes present-tense observation with foreknowledge of their post-collapse futures. The tense handling in Korean is fluid; English rendering should keep the "I know what she will become, and I'm seeing her now" layering clear without making it clunky.
- "기적의 회사" (miracle company) is used mockingly in the story's past and will presumably become ironic/reclaimed in the regression timeline. The first usage in translation should carry the derisive tone.
