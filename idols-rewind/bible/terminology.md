## Blue Sherbet
- Korean term: 블루샤벳
- Category: Group name / organization
- First appearance: 2
- Last Updated: 2026-03-08
- Definition: The girl group managed by Kang Hyuk under his independent agency. Four members: Hee-yeon Lee, Jin-ah Choi, Yoon-jung Hwang, Yumi Cho.
- Usage notes: N/A
- Notes: Post-collapse, the group became a cult phenomenon — individual fancam videos hit 100M+ views; their songs resurface regularly on charts. Referred to by the industry as the output of the "miracle company" (기적의 회사) — intended mockingly, likely to become ironic.

## Miracle Company
- Korean term: 기적의 회사
- Category: Concept / industry label
- First appearance: 3
- Last Updated: 2026-03-08
- Definition: Mocking industry nickname for Kang's agency — the company that had an extraordinary lineup (Hee-yeon, Jin-ah, Yoon-jung, Yumi in one group) and still failed completely.
- Usage notes: Kang notes the label will eventually become positive. The irony is central to the story's premise.

## Comeback / Debut / Activity Period
- Korean term: 활동기간, 데뷔
- Category: Idol industry concept
- First appearance: 2
- Last Updated: 2026-03-08
- Definition: Standard idol industry terms. "Activity period" (활동기간) refers to the promotional cycle around a release. After activity periods end, members enter rest/practice phases.
- Usage notes: Used as in standard K-pop industry context. No story-specific deviation from general usage.

## Monthly Evaluation
- Korean term: 월말평가
- Category: Idol industry concept
- First appearance: 2
- Last Updated: 2026-03-08
- Definition: Regular assessment of trainees' skill levels, typically monthly. Jin-ah jokes that Kang's surprise skill check feels like one.
- Usage notes: Common trainee system term; relevant for conveying the agency's training structure.

## Starex
- Korean term: 스타렉스
- Category: Item (vehicle)
- First appearance: 2
- Last Updated: 2026-03-08
- Definition: Hyundai Starex — a large van, standard vehicle for transporting idol groups. Kang's is described as old/worn.
- Usage notes: Can retain as "Starex" or render as "the van" depending on register. The worn condition is narratively relevant (signals agency's financial state).
