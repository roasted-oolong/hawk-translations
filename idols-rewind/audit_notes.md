## Chapter 5 — Post-Translation Audit

### Issue 1: Dash Rule Violations
- **Category:** Formatting
- **What happened:** The Phase 2 rewrite introduced long dashes in sound effect and action lines (e.g. *Fwsh — thud*). These were not caught during the final review because the audit pass was not active enough — it was treated as a memory check rather than a deliberate search.
- **Pattern:** Formatting violations are most likely to appear in non-dialogue lines — sound effects, action beats, internal monologue — where the rhythm pressure is highest and scrutiny tends to be lowest.
- **Fix applied:** Guidelines updated. Phase 3 editing pass now mandates an active dash search of the full draft, not a reliance on recall.

### Issue 2: Meaning Loss Through Over-Editing
- **Category:** Meaning fidelity
- **What happened:** The closing line was rendered as "The meta breaker had arrived" — preserving the energy of the original while discarding its specific content. The Korean (바야흐로 인터넷 방송계의 생태계 파괴자가 등장했음을 알리는 순간이었다) names the streaming world as the industry affected, frames the moment as a public announcement of arrival, and uses 바야흐로 to signal the dawn of an era. None of that survived into the Phase 2 draft.
- **Pattern:** Thematically significant lines — closing lines, chapter titles, lines with narrative weight — are most vulnerable to this. Phase 2's pressure toward punch and brevity compresses meaning when it should be protecting it.
- **Fix applied:** Guidelines updated. Phase 3 now includes an explicit instruction to give closing lines and thematically significant lines heightened scrutiny during the meaning fidelity check. The closing line failure is used as the documented model example in the guidelines.

### Issue 3: No Dedicated Editing Layer in Original Workflow
- **Category:** Process
- **What happened:** The original workflow moved from Phase 2 (localization rewrite) directly to a final review. The final review was not structured to catch meaning loss or error introduction — only consistency and tone. There was no pass that compared the Phase 2 draft line-by-line against the Korean source.
- **Fix applied:** Phase 3 (Editing Pass — Source Comparison) added to translation_guidelines.md as a mandatory step between Phase 2 and final review. Three-part structure: meaning fidelity, error introduction, dash audit.

---

## Standing Patterns to Watch

- **Sound effect and action lines** are a dash violation risk. Check these first during the dash audit.
- **Closing lines and chapter titles** are a meaning loss risk. Read these against the Korean source with particular care during Phase 3.
- **Phase 2 rewrites of climactic or thematically loaded passages** tend to compress meaning in favor of rhythm. The stronger the instinct to make a line punchy, the more carefully it needs to be checked against the source.
